## Summary

The Silent Witness grammar across the best watched-horror is never about showing a watcher â€” it is about making the world visibly RESPOND to the player's own behavior: teach a baseline, then let deviations, gatekeeping, and records of the player's session carry the surveillance. The single biggest lesson for Project 001: the confirmation climax lands hardest as RETROACTIVE recontextualization â€” evidence that the watching started before the player suspected (The Closing Shift's photo wall, Anatomy's 'your intrusion would be felt', ECHO replaying your own actions) â€” and we can build it almost free, because a server-authoritative game already logs the player's path; replaying that log as door-events, marked floor plans, and footstep audio IS the encounter. Fairness in spot-the-change is a solved problem (Exit 8): small learnable baseline, categorical not pixel-level changes, one binary decision, instant verdict â€” Observation Duty's 1-star reviews document every way to get it wrong. Empirical trust data (Game Studies on Alien: Isolation) proves players accept a watcher that visibly LEARNS but revolt at psychic knowledge, teleporting, and tethering â€” escalating study of the player is the one escalation device our trust law already endorses. Honest gap self-flagged: this is mechanism extraction (~35% toward a buildable spec); it still needs the encounter-design five-act pass, a one-sentence climax lock by the heads, and phone-speaker audio prototyping before anything is 'designed'.

### 4. [ADOPT] Silent Witness, Act II (Discovery) core mechanic; fairness contract for any change-detection in the prototype

### 13. [ADOPT] Audio system, Silent Witness all five acts; template for the studio's sound-is-information doctrine

- **Mechanism:** Sound-only surveillance vocabulary, assembled from the corpus: P.T.'s extra-footstep-and-breathing rider and a radio that addresses you by behavior; DOORS' audio spawn-cue granting a fair grace window; FNAF's audio-channel degradation as escalation; film/game craft consensus that 'a body nearby' is told through breath, cloth, creak, and a footstep where none should be. ## Trust breaks (their haters are our free QA)

- I'm on Observation Duty â€” ambiguous report taxonomy: the same disturbance plausibly classifies as 'ghost', 'intruder', or 'extra object', forcing players to spam guesses against a hidden grader; uncertainty migrates from the fiction into the interface and observation stops mattering (Steam negative reviews / NamuWiki).
- I'm on Observation Duty â€” anomalies spawn off-camera while the player watches another feed, leaving no trace to recover; players lose to information that never existed for them â€” the exact failure our Constitution names as bad design (NamuWiki).
- I'm on Observation Duty â€” first plays are memorization deaths (reviewers report ~5 deaths just learning what rooms normally look like); the game punishes not-yet-knowing instead of teaching the baseline (Steam reviews).
- Observation Duty sequels â€” weak, reused anomalies: 'most of the time nothing happens or anomalies are so weak that you get bored'; when tells stop mattering, players stop believing the world, which kills the paranoia loop at its root (Steam reviews of later entries).
- FNAF 2 / custom nights â€” 'Night 6 comes down to pure luck'; high-difficulty RNG produces unwinnable combinations, so perfect observation ceases to guarantee survival (Markiplier took ~9 hours on 10/20 mode); observation horror dies the moment outcomes decouple from reading the world (Steam forums, GameFAQs).
- Alien: Isolation â€” the three certified trust-killers from academic reception study: perceived psychic knowledge of the player's location, teleporting near hiding spots, rubber-band tethering; each converts fear of the creature into resentment of the developer (gamestudies.org/2002/articles/jaroslav_svelch).
- The Exit 8 â€” 'some anomalies too subtle' (Steam discussions): tells below render-scale readability, plus false-positive turn-backs that reset progress to zero without ever telling the player what they misjudged â€” a costly punishment carrying no lesson.
- Roblox DOORS' Eyes on mobile â€” damage scales with the entity's position on the SCREEN, but small screens keep threats near center and touch camera control is imprecise; the compliance action is hardest on the majority platform, so the same rule is fair on desktop and faintly rigged on a phone (DOORS wiki + design analyses).
- P.T. â€” the final puzzle famously required crowdsourced, borderline-ARG steps (mic input, obscure triggers); the community, not the game, held the survival information â€” legendary mystique for a marketing teaser, but a straight violation of 'all survival information exists somewhere in the world' for a shipped encounter (Wikipedia / P.T. retrospectives).

## Mobile notes

FNAF is the existence proof that observation-horror is a MOBILE-NATIVE genre, not a compromise: static readable scenes, tap-sized interactions, and session compression (mobile nights run ~4.5 minutes with a faster clock) made it a phone phenomenon â€” the Silent Witness arc should likewise complete its unease->confirmation->violation loop within a short-session budget and rebuild baseline literacy fast on re-entry. What survives a small bright screen: categorical, silhouette-scale tells (object added/removed/moved, orientation flipped, light state changed â€” Exit 8's vocabulary), single-room baselines a la P.T.'s corridor, retroactive evidence rooms (a photo wall reads perfectly at 5 inches), and room-coarse gaze aversion. What dies: pixel-level spot-the-change (small screens + outdoor glare + Roblox mobile render scale crush fine detail â€” Exit 8's 'too subtle' complaints get worse, Observation Duty becomes unplayable), screen-position-fine gaze mechanics (DOORS' Eyes is measurably crueler on touch), and any tell living in peripheral vision. Phone speakers cut off roughly below 800Hz-1kHz â€” sub-bass dread and rumble simply do not exist on the majority device â€” so the entire surveillance sound kit must live mid/high band (dry clicks, tape hiss, pencil scratch, breath, footstep transients), which conveniently is exactly the vocabulary this archetype wants; saturation can fake weight if needed. Assume a meaningful slice of young players are effectively muted (noisy rooms, silent phones): every load-bearing audio cue needs a silent sibling â€” the late footstep echo pairs with the reoriented chair, the entry click pairs with the tally marks â€” sound is a channel for the information, never its only container.

## The surprise

The Game Studies reception research on Alien: Isolation found that players who raged at the alien's 'psychic' tracking, teleporting, and tethering raised NO objection to the creature progressively unlocking new behaviors learned from stalking them â€” visible learning was received as fair, even flattering to the fiction. We expected 'it adapts to you' to be the riskiest trust proposition in the archetype; it is empirically the SAFEST escalation device in watched-horror â€” audiences accept a watcher that grows, and revolt only at a watcher that skips its own rules. For a studio whose first law is trust, that means the Silent Witness's core fantasy ('you are being studied') is not merely compatible with fairness â€” it is the one escalation players themselves certify as fair, provided every new capability traces to something the player actually did while observed. Runner-up surprise: the strongest being-watched device in the entire corpus costs zero entities and zero art â€” P.T. attaches the watcher to the player's own footstep audio.
