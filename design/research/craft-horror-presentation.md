########## Lighting & Post-Processing stack for true darkness and dread ##########
SUMMARY: The single most important thing: darkness in Roblox is not one slider, it is FOUR light sources you must all cut at once — the sun/moon (Lighting.Brightness), the skybox acting as a giant area light (EnvironmentDiffuseScale/EnvironmentSpecularScale), the outdoor flat fill (OutdoorAmbient), and the indoor flat fill (Ambient). Your lobby "is still light" because it has an OPEN SKY and Future/ShadowMap default EnvironmentDiffuseScale to 1, so the bright default skybox floods the whole scene with skylight that GlobalShadows cannot block (shadows only stop the direct sun beam, never ambient/environment light) — plus OutdoorAmbient defaults to a bright gray and Brightness to ~2-3. Kill all four (Brightness 0, EnvironmentDiffuseScale 0, EnvironmentSpecularScale 0, Ambient and OutdoorAmbient near-black cold blue), black out the actual Sky instance, then re-introduce light ONLY from placed practical sources so the room becomes a pool of dark with a few readable pools of light. The current blockout fails because it does the opposite: default lighting is designed to make an outdoor scene look pleasant and evenly lit, which is exactly "amateur test level." Then a cold desaturated ColorCorrectionEffect + dark Atmosphere + Bloom-against-black + DepthOfField turns navigable dark into DREAD — but you must tune the final darkness on an actual phone at ~50% brightness, because Roblox tone-maps mobile darker and a scene that is moody on a PC monitor is an unnavigable black smear on a low-end phone.
PRIORITY: 1) Cut the four flooders FIRST (technique 1): Brightness=0, EnvironmentDiffuseScale=0, EnvironmentSpecularScale=0, Ambient=(6,7,12), OutdoorAmbient=(8,10,16), Technology=Future. This alone turns the 'still light' lobby dark and is the direct fix for the lead's complaint — do it before anything else and screenshot the before/after. 2) Black out the Sky instance (technique 2) so the open lobby and every window stop showing a bright sky and stop leaking skylight — cheap, mobile-identical, and it makes step 1 hold up in views of the sky. 3) Re-introduce navigable light with sparse warm practical PointLights + one doorway rim SpotLight + the per-player flashlight (technique 7) so the now-dark scene is dreadful but still readable on a phone. Only AFTER those three does the post-FX grade matter — layer ColorCorrection (cold desaturated), Atmosphere (dark cold fog), Bloom (Threshold 1.0), and DoF, then final-tune the whole stack on a real low-end phone at ~50% brightness.
PITFALLS:
  - Only lowering Lighting.Brightness and expecting darkness — the skybox (EnvironmentDiffuseScale, default 1 in Future/ShadowMap) and OutdoorAmbient keep flooding the open-sky lobby with even light. This is the exact bug in the current build: it reads 'still light' because three of the four light sources are untouched.
  - Leaving EnvironmentDiffuseScale/EnvironmentSpecularScale at their Future/ShadowMap default of 1, so the bright default skybox acts as a giant ambient area light that GlobalShadows cannot block (shadows only stop the direct sun beam, never ambient/environment light).
  - Trusting GlobalShadows or a roof to darken a room while the sky is still the default bright gradient and EnvironmentDiffuseScale=1 — ambient sky light leaks in and there is a visibly bright sky in every window, which reads as daytime and kills dread instantly.
  - Relying on ClockTime=0 (midnight) to create darkness — the moon and default skybox still light the scene; ClockTime is nearly irrelevant once you black out the Sky and set Brightness/EnvironmentDiffuseScale to 0.
  - Making the scene pitch-black with zero practical lights — this violates the horror-craft rule (darkness so total it is unreadable is banned) and, combined with Roblox's darker mobile tone-mapping, leaves ~70% of players literally unable to navigate. Fear needs silhouettes and rim light, not a black screen.
  - Setting Atmosphere.Color (or FogColor) to a bright value — a glowing fog wall LIGHTENS the scene and looks foggy-pretty, not dreadful. Horror fog must be dark and cold and eat light.
  - Tuning the entire look in the bright Studio viewport / on a PC monitor and never testing on a real low-end phone — Roblox tone-maps mobile darker, so a moody PC scene becomes an unnavigable black smear on the platform 70% of players actually use.
  - Stacking too many negative-brightness effects (ExposureCompensation -1+, ColorCorrection.Brightness -0.2, dense fog) so the frame becomes uniform mud with no contrast — dread needs deep blacks NEXT TO readable light pools, not a globally dim gray wash.
  - Enabling Shadows=true on many local lights under Future — it tanks FPS on mobile and Roblox will demote it anyway; budget 1-2 shadow-casting lights total (ideally just the flashlight) and keep the rest Shadows=false.
  - Adding SunRaysEffect or a bright Bloom Threshold out of habit — with no sun and a dark frame, SunRays does nothing useful and a low Bloom Threshold blooms the whole frame into a hazy glow that undoes the darkness.
TECHNIQUES:
  * Cut the four light-flooders at the root (this is THE fix for 'it's still light')
    API: Lighting.Brightness, Lighting.EnvironmentDiffuseScale, Lighting.EnvironmentSpecularScale, Lighting.Ambient, Lighting.OutdoorAmbient, Lighting.ExposureCompensation, Lighting.GlobalShadows, Lighting.Technology, Lighting.ClockTime
    VALUES: Technology = Enum.Technology.Future (mobile fallback: ShadowMap). Brightness = 0 for pure interiors, or 0.3 max if you want a faint exterior source. EnvironmentDiffuseScale = 0. EnvironmentSpecularScale = 0. Ambient = Color3.fromRGB(6, 7, 12). OutdoorAmbient = Color3.fromRGB(8, 10, 16). ExposureCompensation = -0.25 on PC. GlobalShadows = true. ClockTime = 0 (irrelevant once the sky is black, but set it). Key relationship: effective outdoor ambient is the per-channel max(Ambient, OutdoorAmbient), so BOTH must be dark or the higher one wins and re-floods the scene.
    GAMES: DOORS runs Future is Bright with near-black ambient and Brightness near 0, so every visible photon comes from room lamps and the entity — the corridor between lights is genuinely black. Pressure (Hadal Blacksite) crushes ambient to near-zero cold blue so the flashlight cone is the only reliable vision. Rooms/Apeirophobia corridors are lit almost exclusively by sparse practical lights over a black ambient floor.
    MOBILE: Future's per-local-light shadows are the expensive part; the ambient/Brightness cuts themselves are free on any device. On low-end phones Roblox auto-demotes Future toward ShadowMap, so do NOT rely on local-light shadows for readability. Because mobile tone-maps darker, hold ExposureCompensation near 0 (not -0.25) on detected touch devices, or expose a client brightness slider — a -0.25 that looks moody on PC can push a cheap phone to unnavigable.
  * Black out the actual sky so the skybox stops being a light source
    API: Sky (instance parented to Lighting): SkyboxUp/Down/Left/Right/Front/Back, CelestialBodiesShown, StarCount, SunAngularSize, MoonAngularSize; combined with the EnvironmentDiffuseScale=0 from technique 1
    VALUES: Insert a Sky under Lighting. Set all six SkyboxUp/Down/Lf/Rt/Ft/Bk faces to a pure-black texture (upload a 2x2 black PNG and reuse its rbxassetid on all six). CelestialBodiesShown = false. StarCount = 0. SunAngularSize = 0. MoonAngularSize = 0. This handles anywhere the player can SEE the sky (windows, the open lobby, doorways) — EnvironmentDiffuseScale=0 stops the sky lighting surfaces, and a black Sky stops the sky being a visibly bright hole in the geometry.
    GAMES: The Mimic and Apeirophobia use flat dark/black skyboxes so exterior views read as oppressive void, not open air. DOORS' windowed rooms show near-black exteriors so a window is a threat vector, never a light source or an escape.
    MOBILE: A 2x2 black texture is the cheapest possible skybox — zero mobile cost and it renders identically on every tier. This is one of the few darkness techniques that is 100% consistent PC-to-phone, which makes it a reliability anchor: lean on blacked-out sky + practical lights rather than on subtle ambient values that mobile tone-mapping can shift.
  * Atmosphere for claustrophobic depth and a dread horizon
    API: Atmosphere (instance parented to Lighting): Density, Offset, Color, Decay, Glare, Haze; optional legacy Lighting.FogEnd/FogStart/FogColor as a low-end fallback
    VALUES: Insert Atmosphere under Lighting. Density = 0.42 (interiors 0.30, open lobby up to 0.50). Offset = 0.25. Color = Color3.fromRGB(60, 66, 78) (cold desaturated gray-blue — keep it DARK; a bright Color makes a glowing fog wall that lightens the scene). Decay = Color3.fromRGB(15, 16, 22). Glare = 0. Haze = 1.8. Legacy fallback for lowest tiers where Atmosphere is weak: Lighting.FogEnd = 90, FogStart = 12, FogColor = Color3.fromRGB(7, 8, 12).
    GAMES: DOORS' signature look is heavy fog swallowing the corridor a few rooms ahead, forcing you forward blind. Pressure uses dense dark atmosphere to sell crushing-depth void. The Mimic uses fog to hide room extents and stage partial-glimpse reveals.
    MOBILE: Atmosphere is cheaper than dense legacy Fog and scales well, but low-end phones render it thinner — so pair it with the legacy FogEnd fallback above so distance is still hidden if Atmosphere is demoted. Do not push Density past ~0.5 on mobile or near-field readability collapses on a small screen; verify the flashlight cone still cuts through it on a phone.
  * ColorCorrectionEffect — the cold, desaturated horror grade
    API: ColorCorrectionEffect (parented to Lighting): Enabled, Brightness, Contrast, Saturation, TintColor
    VALUES: Insert ColorCorrectionEffect under Lighting. Enabled = true. Brightness = -0.06. Contrast = 0.18. Saturation = -0.40. TintColor = Color3.fromRGB(198, 214, 236) (cold blue). For a sicker, more clinical Threshold-place feel push Saturation to -0.55 and TintColor toward Color3.fromRGB(206, 214, 210) (dead green-gray). Keep Brightness only slightly negative — this effect multiplies the whole frame, so a big negative here crushes your practical lights into mud on mobile.
    GAMES: The Mimic and Grace lean on heavily desaturated cold grades. Specter and Pressure use cold blue/teal tints to feel clinical and airless. DOORS keeps saturation low so the gold of a room lamp and the entity's accent colors pop against a drained frame.
    MOBILE: ColorCorrection is one of the cheapest post effects and renders on all tiers. The risk is stacking negative Brightness here on top of a scene that mobile already tone-maps darker — keep ColorCorrection.Brightness >= -0.08 and do the cold/desaturated work with Saturation and TintColor, not by darkening. Test the grade on a phone; blue tints in particular shift on cheap panels.
  * BloomEffect — make every practical light glow against the black
    API: BloomEffect (parented to Lighting): Enabled, Intensity, Size, Threshold
    VALUES: Insert BloomEffect under Lighting. Enabled = true. Intensity = 0.6. Size = 40. Threshold = 1.0. Tuning: LOWER Threshold (toward 0.8) = more surfaces bloom; keep it around 1.0 so only actual light sources and lit surfaces bloom, not the whole frame. For a single hero light (a flickering bulb, a distant doorway) you can localize the effect by raising that light's Brightness rather than lowering global Threshold.
    GAMES: DOORS' room lamps and the Screech/entity lights bloom softly out of the dark, pulling your eye. Pressure's sparse emergency lights and the flashlight beam bloom against the void. Rooms uses bloomed practical fixtures as the only landmarks in otherwise black corridors.
    MOBILE: Bloom is moderately cheap and renders on most tiers but is one of the first effects low-end devices soften — do not depend on bloom alone to make a light readable; the light's actual Range/Brightness must carry it. Keep Size <= ~48 on mobile so the halo does not smear across a small screen and wash out nearby geometry.
  * DepthOfField — near-field dread and a murky far distance
    API: DepthOfFieldEffect (parented to Lighting): Enabled, FocusDistance, InFocusRadius, NearIntensity, FarIntensity
    VALUES: Insert DepthOfFieldEffect under Lighting. Enabled = true. FocusDistance = 18. InFocusRadius = 22. NearIntensity = 0. FarIntensity = 0.6. This keeps the player's immediate walkable area sharp while everything far hazes into murk, compounding the Atmosphere. For a specific 'something is right in your face' beat, briefly script NearIntensity up to ~0.5 so the foreground blurs and the player loses spatial certainty.
    GAMES: Grace and Specter use DoF heavily for a cinematic, uneasy frame. The Mimic blurs far geometry to stage partial-glimpse reveals (silhouette before full sight). Many cutscene/reveal moments in DOORS-style games spike near-field blur to sell disorientation.
    MOBILE: DoF is on the pricier side and low-end phones may soften or drop it, so treat it as enhancement, not as a load-bearing way to hide threats — Atmosphere + black must already hide the distance. Keep default NearIntensity at 0 so the near field stays crisp on a small bright screen where blur would make walking nauseating; only spike it for brief scripted moments.
  * Navigable darkness: practical lights, rim light, and the mobile brightness floor
    API: PointLight / SpotLight (parented to source Parts, not to Lighting): Brightness, Range, Color, Shadows, Angle, Face; SpotLight.Shadows requires Lighting.Technology=Future; per-player flashlight SpotLight welded to camera/Head; skip SunRaysEffect (no sun)
    VALUES: Sparse practical PointLights: Brightness = 1.2, Range = 14, Color = Color3.fromRGB(255, 236, 200) (warm tungsten), Shadows = false on most, Shadows = true on only 1-2 hero lights (Future only). Doorway/window SpotLight to rim-light silhouettes: Angle = 55, Range = 30, Brightness = 2, Face = Front. Per-player flashlight SpotLight (client, welded to camera): Angle = 45, Range = 60, Brightness = 2.5, Color = Color3.fromRGB(255, 244, 214), Shadows = true (cap this as the one guaranteed shadow-caster). SunRaysEffect: leave OUT (no sun); only add at Intensity 0.15, Spread 0.4 if a single bright window source exists.
    GAMES: DOORS, Rooms, and Apeirophobia are almost entirely 'black corridor + sparse warm practical lights + flashlight' — the practicals are the only navigation. Pressure's flashlight-as-primary-vision plus rare emergency lights is the model for your per-player client light. The Mimic backlights entities so you see a silhouette before the full reveal.
    MOBILE: This is the MOST mobile-critical technique: because Roblox renders mobile darker, ~70% of your players see less than you do, so readability must come from placed lights, not from ambient. Keep practical Range/Brightness generous enough that a phone at 50% brightness can still navigate between pools, cap Shadows=true to 1-2 lights total (per-local-light shadows are Future-only and the biggest mobile cost), and ALWAYS final-tune darkness on a real low-end phone, not the Studio viewport. Consider a client-side ExposureCompensation nudge (+0.25 to +0.4) or a brightness slider for detected touch devices.

########## The flashlight — the core horror navigation mechanic (client-side moving light cone in enforced darkness) ##########
SUMMARY: The single most important thing: a flashlight is not a light, it is a MASK — a tight moving cone that reveals a sliver while the enforced darkness hides everything else, so the player only sees what they choose to point at and dread lives in the un-pointed-at 95%. Our current build fails this at the root: it is globally lit (high Ambient/OutdoorAmbient, daytime ClockTime, sky fill), so there is no darkness for a cone to carve — a flashlight added on top would be invisible and pointless. The fix is a two-part contract that must ship together: (1) crush the whole Lighting service to near-black with Technology=Future so a SpotLight can cast real local shadows, and (2) give each player a client-side SpotLight cone on a camera-following anchor part with Shadows=true. Critically, because Roblox renders noticeably darker on mobile (auto-low graphics also drops local light shadows entirely on the ~70% mobile base), the darkness and the cone must be tuned to stay NAVIGABLE on a bright phone screen — that means a brighter SpotLight and a small exposure nudge on mobile, verified on a real low-end phone, not eyeballed on a PC monitor. This dovetails with our "being watched" concept for free: the cone limits what the PLAYER sees, while entity AI on the server knows the player's exact position regardless of where the light points — you are blind, the room is not.
PRIORITY: Do these three, in this exact order — each is worthless without the one before it. (1) KILL THE GLOBAL LIGHT FIRST. In the Lighting service set Technology=Future, Ambient=Color3.fromRGB(4,4,6), OutdoorAmbient=Color3.fromRGB(6,6,10), Brightness=1, ClockTime=0, EnvironmentDiffuseScale=0, EnvironmentSpecularScale=0, and add an Atmosphere (Density 0.35, Haze 1.6). This alone converts 'amateur test level' into 'genuinely dark place' in five minutes and is the precondition for everything else. (2) ADD THE CLIENT-SIDE SPOTLIGHT CONE. Drop the paste-ready RenderStepped+Lerp LocalScript (SpotLight Angle=45, Range=55, Brightness=4, Shadows=true, warm/cold Color) on a massless camera-following anchor part, wired to a ContextActionService toggle with an auto mobile button. Now the player has the actual flashlight and the darkness has meaning. (3) MAKE IT SURVIVE A PHONE + ADD ONE FLICKER BEAT. Apply the mobile profile (Brightness 6, ExposureCompensation +0.35) and verify navigability on a real low-end Android at auto graphics, then wire a single server-fired flicker on your first scare. After these three, the blockout reads as a dark, dreadful, real place — not a gray test level.
PITFALLS:
  - THE CURRENT BUILD'S CORE BUG: globally lit gray blockout — high Ambient/OutdoorAmbient, Brightness up, daytime ClockTime, sky fill on. There is no darkness, so a flashlight added on top is invisible and meaningless. Nothing else in this dimension matters until the Lighting service is crushed to near-black.
  - Using a PointLight instead of a SpotLight for the player light — it emits an omnidirectional glow bubble that reveals 360° around the player, reads as a lamp/aura not a torch, and destroys the entire 'you only see what you point at' mechanic.
  - Leaving Technology on Voxel or ShadowMap — neither casts shadows from local lights, so the SpotLight lights surfaces flatly with no shape. Only Enum.Technology.Future casts per-pixel shadows from a SpotLight. (Corollary: forgetting to set SpotLight.Shadows=true.)
  - Leaving EnvironmentDiffuseScale/EnvironmentSpecularScale at default — the sky keeps secretly fill-lighting the interior so 'dark' rooms stay grey. Set both to 0.
  - Parenting the light to HumanoidRootPart (the whole character spins to face the aim) or burying the SpotLight inside the Head mesh (it backfaces the skull / hits a wall behind the player).
  - Driving the cone with a `while wait()` loop — caps ~30Hz and produces a visibly jittery, laggy beam. Use RunService.RenderStepped. And with NO smoothing the beam snaps robotically; a small Lerp gives it hand-held weight.
  - Updating the flashlight on the SERVER or replicating it to everyone — causes lag and cost. The cone must be a per-player LocalScript; only the battery/flicker TRIGGERS are server-authoritative.
  - Tuning darkness on a PC monitor and shipping it — Roblox renders darker on mobile AND auto-lowers graphics on weak phones (which also disables local-light shadows). ~70% of players then get an unnavigable black screen. Must bump Brightness/ExposureCompensation on mobile and test on a real cheap phone.
  - A single Neon-material or high-Brightness self-lit part left in the blockout — it glows through the darkness and breaks the whole contrast contract that the flashlight depends on.
  - Cone geometry wrong: Angle 90+ or Range near-max-wide reveals the whole room (no dread); Angle <30 or Range <25 is claustrophobic to the point of unplayable, especially on a small mobile screen. Stay ~40-50 Angle, ~50-55 Range.
  - Hard battery drain with fast depletion — turns a psychological horror into a resource-management chore, and punishes mobile players whose touch-toggle misfires. If included, make it soft and never hard-cut the beam to pure black (floor it at ~15%).
  - Forgetting the anchor part hygiene: not setting CanCollide=false / Massless=true / CanQuery=false means the light part physically drags or trips the character and shows up in raycasts.
TECHNIQUES:
  * The emitter: a SpotLight on a dedicated anchor part — never a PointLight, never on HumanoidRootPart
    API: Instance SpotLight (properties: Face, Angle, Range, Brightness, Color, Shadows, Enabled) parented to a purpose-made Part (Transparency=1, CanCollide=false, CanQuery=false, Massless=true, CastShadow=false) inside the Character model
    VALUES: SpotLight.Face = Enum.NormalId.Front • Angle = 45 (full cone degrees; tighten to 40 for more dread, 50 max before it stops feeling like a torch) • Range = 55 (Roblox hard-caps Range at 60, so you are near max on purpose) • Brightness = 4 (raise to 5-6 for mobile, see mobile technique) • Color = Color3.fromRGB(255, 244, 214) warm tungsten • Shadows = true • Enabled = false at spawn. Anchor part: Size Vector3.new(0.2,0.2,0.2), Transparency=1, CanCollide=false, CanQuery=false, Massless=true, CastShadow=false. Do NOT use PointLight — it emits an omni glow bubble that reads as a lamp/aura, kills all directionality, and reveals 360° around the player, destroying the 'you only see what you point at' rule. Do NOT parent the light to HumanoidRootPart (character spins with lookAt) or bury it inside the Head mesh (backfaces the skull).
    GAMES: DOORS' Rechargeable Flashlight and Pressure's Flashlight are both cone emitters (warm-ish in DOORS, cold clinical white in Pressure). Rooms and Apeirophobia (Backrooms) both give a single directional player cone as the only reliable light source. None of the top horror games use a bare PointLight as the player's main light.
    MOBILE: SpotLight with Shadows=true is the single heaviest part of this system on phones. Keep exactly ONE player cone (never one-per-limb). On low-end, local-light shadows silently do not render (auto graphics <= ~4), so the cone still lights but stops casting — acceptable degradation, the beam is still navigable. Bump Brightness to 5-6 on mobile so the cone still punches through the darker mobile render.
  * Client-side camera-follow with RenderStepped + Lerp smoothing (paste-ready)
    API: RunService.RenderStepped, workspace.CurrentCamera, CFrame.lookAt, CFrame:Lerp — all in a LocalScript under StarterPlayerScripts (per-player, never server-updated)
    VALUES: Paste-ready LocalScript body:

local RunService = game:GetService("RunService")
local plr = game.Players.LocalPlayer
local cam = workspace.CurrentCamera
local char = plr.Character or plr.CharacterAdded:Wait()
local head = char:WaitForChild("Head")
local anchor = Instance.new("Part")
anchor.Size = Vector3.new(0.2,0.2,0.2); anchor.Transparency = 1
anchor.CanCollide = false; anchor.CanQuery = false
anchor.Massless = true; anchor.CastShadow = false; anchor.Parent = char
local light = Instance.new("SpotLight")
light.Face = Enum.NormalId.Front; light.Angle = 45; light.Range = 55
light.Brightness = 4; light.Color = Color3.fromRGB(255,244,214)
light.Shadows = true; light.Enabled = false; light.Parent = anchor
local smoothed = cam.CFrame
RunService.RenderStepped:Connect(function(dt)
    smoothed = smoothed:Lerp(cam.CFrame, math.clamp(dt*12, 0, 1))
    anchor.CFrame = CFrame.lookAt(head.Position, head.Position + smoothed.LookVector)
end)

Tuning knob: the dt*12 factor is the 'weight' of the light. 12 = snappy; drop to 6-8 for a heavier, laggier beam that trails the camera (reads like a real handheld torch and adds unease). For over-the-shoulder instead of first-person, replace cam.CFrame.LookVector with (plr:GetMouse().Hit.Position - head.Position).
    GAMES: Apeirophobia's beam has visible follow-jitter that developers openly cite as the reference feel; Pressure and Rooms both smooth the cone with a slight delay so it lags the camera. The Roblox community 'Advanced Flashlight' modules explicitly recommend RenderStepped + Lerp/Tween over while-loops to kill jitter.
    MOBILE: RenderStepped follows the phone's real framerate, so on a 30fps device the math.clamp(dt*12) auto-compensates (larger dt = faster catch-up) and the beam stays glued to look direction — no per-platform code needed. One Lerp per frame is free on any phone.
  * Crush the Lighting service to near-black — the darkness that makes the cone mean anything
    API: Lighting service (Technology, Ambient, OutdoorAmbient, Brightness, ClockTime, GlobalShadows, ExposureCompensation, EnvironmentDiffuseScale, EnvironmentSpecularScale) + an Atmosphere instance under Lighting
    VALUES: Lighting.Technology = Enum.Technology.Future (REQUIRED — only Future casts per-pixel shadows from SpotLights; Voxel/ShadowMap ignore local-light shadows and the beam goes flat) • Ambient = Color3.fromRGB(4,4,6) • OutdoorAmbient = Color3.fromRGB(6,6,10) • Brightness = 1 • ClockTime = 0 • GlobalShadows = true • EnvironmentDiffuseScale = 0 and EnvironmentSpecularScale = 0 (kills the sky's free ambient fill so interiors go genuinely black) • ExposureCompensation = 0 on PC. Add an Atmosphere: Density = 0.35, Offset = 0.25, Color = Color3.fromRGB(60,58,66), Decay = Color3.fromRGB(38,38,46), Glare = 0, Haze = 1.6 (haze makes the cone's light visibly scatter and swallows far geometry into murk). Also ensure NO parts are Material=Neon or high-Brightness self-lit — a single glowing Neon panel ruins the whole darkness contract.
    GAMES: DOORS runs near-black rooms on Future so the flashlight and lightning flashes are the only reveals. Pressure's Blacksite corridors are pitch black outside your cone. Rooms and Apeirophobia both rely on aggressive fog/atmosphere so the void beyond the beam reads as infinite.
    MOBILE: Future is heavier than ShadowMap but is the only technology that makes the cone cast shadows; keep it but pair with the mobile-brightness technique. Atmosphere/Haze is cheap on mobile (screen-space) and actually helps hide low-detail blockout geometry. WARNING: mobile auto-graphics render this darker than your PC — do not judge darkness on a monitor; see mobile technique for the exposure nudge that keeps it navigable.
  * Toggle input: ContextActionService with an auto-generated mobile touch button + debounce
    API: ContextActionService:BindAction (createTouchButton=true auto-spawns the mobile button), :SetTitle, :SetPosition, Enum.KeyCode.F, Enum.KeyCode.ButtonR2, Enum.UserInputState.Begin
    VALUES: local CAS = game:GetService("ContextActionService")
local on, last = false, 0
local function toggle(_, state)
    if state ~= Enum.UserInputState.Begin then return end
    if tick() - last < 0.35 then return end   -- debounce fat-fingers
    last = tick(); on = not on; light.Enabled = on
end
CAS:BindAction("Flashlight", toggle, true, Enum.KeyCode.F, Enum.KeyCode.ButtonR2)
CAS:SetTitle("Flashlight", "LIGHT")
CAS:SetPosition("Flashlight", UDim2.new(1, -140, 0.5, -30))

The `true` third arg is createTouchButton — it auto-draws the on-screen mobile button, no separate GUI needed. Bind F (keyboard) and ButtonR2 (controller) in the same call. Position the touch button on the right side, thumb-reachable, clear of the movement thumbstick (left) and the jump button (bottom-right) — UDim2.new(1,-140,0.5,-30) puts it mid-right.
    GAMES: Every top Roblox horror game ships an on-screen mobile button because ~70%+ of players are on phones; DOORS, Pressure, and Apeirophobia all expose their light/interact via ContextActionService-style touch buttons. Pressure uses a held toggle; DOORS ties the light to an equippable item.
    MOBILE: This IS the mobile-safe path — createTouchButton draws a correctly-sized, Roblox-native touch target automatically and it scales per device. Just verify placement does not overlap the default jump button on small screens (test on a 5-inch phone).
  * Flicker as a directed scare beat + SOFT battery (recommendation: yes flicker, go easy on battery)
    API: Server: RemoteEvent (FlashlightFlicker) fired from server for authored beats; a NumberValue 'Battery' (0-100) owned server-side. Client: TweenService or a task.wait() coroutine driving SpotLight.Brightness / .Enabled
    VALUES: Flicker (client, triggered by server RemoteEvent so scares are authored & server-authoritative):

local base = light.Brightness
local function flicker(dur)
    local t0 = tick()
    while tick() - t0 < dur do
        light.Brightness = (math.random() < 0.5) and base*0.05 or base
        task.wait(math.random(3,9)/100)   -- 0.03-0.09s stutter
    end
    light.Brightness = base
end
-- FlashlightFlicker.OnClientEvent:Connect(function(d) flicker(d) end)

Soft battery (server owns the number; drain only while ON): drain 1.5%/sec = ~66s of continuous light per full charge (matches Pressure's ~63s-per-battery feel). Below 20% start a slow dim (Brightness lerps toward base*0.5) + occasional involuntary flicker; at 0% do NOT hard-cut to pure black — drop to base*0.15 so a mobile player is never fully stranded. Recharge via battery pickups (server validates pickup, sets Battery). RECOMMENDATION for The Threshold: include FLICKER (it is pure dread and costs nothing), but make battery SOFT or optional — hard drain turns a psychological horror into a resource-management chore and punishes mobile players whose toggles misfire. If you want DOORS' model instead: no consumable battery, the light slowly self-recharges when off.
    GAMES: Pressure's battery drains (~63s per battery) and has a hand-cranked backup flashlight for when you run dry; DOORS' flashlight recharges over time rather than consuming items; scripted flicker-on-scare beats are standard in The Mimic and Specter. The universal lesson: flicker = free dread, hard battery = optional and easy to overdo.
    MOBILE: Flicker is just Brightness writes — free on mobile. Server-authoritative battery/flicker triggers mean no exploit and consistent timing across devices. Never fully black-out on a phone: the mobile screen is already darker, so a true 0-brightness state can leave a phone player genuinely unable to navigate — always floor the beam at ~15%.
  * Mobile survival: keep it navigable on a dark, low-end phone screen
    API: UserInputService.TouchEnabled or GetLastInputType() for platform detect; Lighting.ExposureCompensation; SpotLight.Brightness; SpotLight.Shadows; StarterGui / graphics-aware tuning
    VALUES: On detected touch/low-end clients, apply a mobile profile at spawn: SpotLight.Brightness = 6 (vs 4 on PC) • Lighting.ExposureCompensation = +0.35 (vs 0 on PC) — this counteracts the documented fact that Roblox renders DARKER on mobile and that auto-graphics-quality drops the render further. Detect with: `local isMobile = game:GetService("UserInputService").TouchEnabled`. Because local-light shadows silently stop rendering when auto graphics falls to ~4 or below, do NOT rely on shadow-shape for readability — the beam's brightness alone must define the space. Keep the beam Range at 55 but consider Angle 48 on mobile (slightly wider) so the smaller screen still shows enough of the corridor to walk. GOLDEN RULE: never sign off darkness on a PC monitor — load the place on a real cheap Android phone at default (auto) graphics and confirm you can find a doorway with the beam.
    GAMES: DOORS is explicitly tuned to remain playable on phones despite its darkness — its huge mobile playerbase forces it; Pressure and Apeirophobia likewise stay navigable on mobile. Studios that ship PC-only darkness get flooded with 'too dark, can't see' reviews from mobile.
    MOBILE: This technique IS the mobile-safety layer. The +0.35 ExposureCompensation and Brightness 6 are the concrete knobs. Test matrix minimum: one 3-4GB-RAM Android on auto graphics, one mid iPhone — if you can navigate a room on both, ship it.
  * The cone as the 'being watched' engine — plus warm/cold color and near-field fill
    API: SpotLight.Color (warm vs cold) • a second dim PointLight for near-field fill • entity AI server-side (distance checks / raycasts) that ignore the player's light • SpotLight.Range vs entity aggro range
    VALUES: COLOR: warm tungsten Color3.fromRGB(255,244,214) reads human/vulnerable/handheld (DOORS-like); cold Color3.fromRGB(214,232,255) reads clinical/institutional/wrong (Pressure-like). For 'The Threshold: impossible, indifferent place' I recommend cold-neutral Color3.fromRGB(232,238,248) — a light that does not comfort. NEAR-FIELD FILL (optional, stops pure-black feet): add a PointLight to the same anchor, Range=8, Brightness=0.4, Color matched, Shadows=false — a faint pool at the player's body so they aren't blind at their own feet while everything beyond the cone stays black. BEING-WATCHED CONTRACT: entity AI on the SERVER detects the player by distance/raycast to HumanoidRootPart and does NOT care where the flashlight points — set SpotLight.Range = 55 but let entity detection range be LARGER (e.g. 70-90 studs) so the thing sees you before your beam can ever reach it. Optionally make pointing the beam AT an entity a trigger (it reacts / advances) so the light becomes a liability.
    GAMES: DOORS' Rush and Ambush give near-zero visual warning — you react to audio/environment, not sight, because your light can't reach them in time; Pressure's Eyefestation punishes you for LOOKING (the light/gaze becomes the danger); Specter and The Mimic build encounters around the ghost knowing your position while you fumble a limited cone. The asymmetry — you see a sliver, it sees everything — is the shared spine of Roblox horror.
    MOBILE: The near-field fill PointLight has Shadows=false and Range=8, so it is nearly free on mobile and, importantly, keeps a phone player from being totally blind at their feet on a dark screen. Server-side entity detection is platform-agnostic and unexploitable. Cold vs warm color is zero cost.

########## Materials, texture, and cheap dressing — making gray blockout read as a real oppressive place ##########
SUMMARY: The single most important thing is to give every surface a real material identity and a differentiated cold palette — the current build fails because it is the Roblox Studio default: one uniform medium gray (~Color3.fromRGB(163,162,165)) on default Slate/SmoothPlastic, flat uninterrupted wall planes, right angles everywhere, no trim, no grime, all fully lit. That combination is the literal visual signature of an untouched blockout, which is exactly why it reads as an amateur test level. Real space reads as real because light catches differentiated materials at differentiated roughness across dozens of subtly different values — not because of asset budget. DOORS proves this: its Retro Mode strips the dressing and the same geometry instantly looks like a Roblox blockout, so the material + palette + dressing layer is doing ~80% of the "this is a real place" work for near-zero polygon cost. The fix ships today with zero imported assets: assign concrete/wood/metal/brick per surface, jitter a desaturated sickly palette, add baseboard/reveal geometry, drop the ceiling, and layer 3-4 reusable grime decals plus a handful of kitbash props. On mobile this is nearly free — built-in materials cost no extra memory — but tune albedo values slightly brighter than instinct says because Roblox renders darker on phones.
PRIORITY: 1) MATERIALS + PALETTE FIRST (highest leverage, zero asset cost, ship in one pass): assign real materials per surface (Concrete/Wood/Metal/Plaster — ban SmoothPlastic and Neon), then replace the single flat gray with the desaturated cold/sickly palette AND jitter every part's Color by +/-6-10 RGB so no two adjacent parts match. This alone flips the scene from 'blockout' to 'a place' before you touch lighting. 2) PROPORTION + TRIM SECOND: drop ceilings to 8-9 studs, tighten corridors to 6-9 wide, and add baseboard + door casing + reveal/panel-line geometry to break every flat plane and create shadow-catching edges. 3) GRIME + DRESSING THIRD: layer a reusable 3-4 decal grime kit (tinted/rotated) where dirt physically collects, plus a handful of off-grid kitbash props with one story-implying beat per room (tipped chair, dropped flashlight). Do 1 before 2 before 3 — dressing a scene that still has flat gray SmoothPlastic walls is polishing the wrong layer.
PITFALLS:
  - Every part is the identical default medium gray (~Color3.fromRGB(163,162,165)) — this exact value IS the untouched-blockout signature; the eye recognizes it instantly as 'nobody has dressed this yet'.
  - Everything is SmoothPlastic or default Slate — no physical material identity, so light hits every surface the same flat matte way and the whole scene reads as one plasticky mesh. SmoothPlastic on architecture is the single loudest amateur tell.
  - Flat, uninterrupted wall planes with no baseboard, trim, reveal or panel line — no edges to catch light or shadow, no scale reference, so walls read as abstract boxes not architecture.
  - Perfectly clean surfaces with zero grime, stains, cracks or wear — uncanny; a real abandoned place is streaked and dirty, and cleanliness screams 'brand-new blockout'.
  - Uniform color across every part — no per-part value jitter — so light has nothing to grade across and large surfaces look like a single flat card instead of many aged surfaces.
  - Obvious texture tiling — same tile at same rotation repeating in one sightline with no grime overlay or StudsPerTile tuning to hide the seam.
  - Ceilings too tall (15-20 studs) and rooms too wide — no confinement pressure, so the space feels like an empty gymnasium test level rather than oppressive horror.
  - Everything on a perfect 90-degree grid with identical part sizes and no off-angle props — too orthogonal and too clean, which never happens in a real used space.
  - Neon used for light sources or as a material on architecture — reads as gamey/Roblox-y and destroys the grounded illusion instantly.
  - Crushing albedo values too dark (0.10-0.15) to compensate for wanting 'dark' — on mobile (which renders darker) the level becomes unnavigable; darkness must come from Lighting while albedo stays readable.
  - One material used everywhere (all Slate, or all Concrete) — real spaces layer 4-6 materials; monotony of material reads as low-effort even if the material itself is fine.
TECHNIQUES:
  * Assign real physical materials per surface — get off default Slate/SmoothPlastic/Plastic
    API: BasePart.Material (Enum.Material.Concrete / Brick / WoodPlanks / Metal / Plaster / Fabric / Granite), BasePart.Reflectance, BasePart.CastShadow, MaterialService.Use2022Materials
    VALUES: Layer 4-6 materials per room, never one. Walls: Concrete or Plaster. Floor: Concrete (corridors) or WoodPlanks (rooms). Trim/doors: WoodPlanks or Metal. Pipes/fixtures: Metal or DiamondPlate. Wet/rusted accents: CorrodedMetal. Keep Reflectance at 0 for dry concrete/plaster; only 0.03-0.08 on metal/wet floors, never higher (higher reads plasticky). Ensure Use2022Materials is ON (MaterialService) so materials have real PBR grain instead of the flat legacy look. BAN Neon and SmoothPlastic on architecture entirely — SmoothPlastic is the amateur tell. Bonus: setting Material=Concrete vs WoodPlanks also swaps default footstep/material sounds, so audio-visual grounding is free.
    GAMES: DOORS layers marble, wood plank floors, brick, wallpaper, and metal in a single hotel room; Pressure (Urbanshade) leans on Metal/DiamondPlate/CorrodedMetal + Concrete for its industrial facility; Apeirophobia/Rooms use Carpet + Plaster + drop-ceiling metal fixtures for liminal spaces.
    MOBILE: Built-in materials add ZERO texture memory beyond what the engine already loads — this is the most mobile-safe technique that exists. No per-platform change needed except keeping Reflectance low so mobile's harsher specular doesn't blow out.
  * Desaturated cold/sickly palette with per-part value jitter — kill the single flat gray
    API: BasePart.Color / Color3.fromRGB, applied per-part; verify against Lighting.ColorCorrectionEffect.Saturation
    VALUES: Ban the default Color3.fromRGB(163,162,165). Palette (fromRGB): Concrete wall base (94,97,92); Sickly institutional plaster (122,120,104); Ceiling — DARKER than walls so it looms (56,58,58); Floor concrete (70,72,71); Dark wood trim/baseboard (58,50,42); Cold steel (86,92,97); Rust accent (99,64,44); Institutional teal accent (64,90,92). RULE: keep HSV Saturation <= 0.12 and Value 0.22-0.45. CRITICAL anti-flatness move: jitter every wall/floor part's Color by +/-6 to 10 RGB points from the base so no two adjacent parts are identical — this alone breaks the 'one giant mesh' look and gives light something to grade across. Sickly reads = nudge hue toward yellow-green (jaundice); dread-cold reads = nudge toward blue-green.
    GAMES: DOORS shifts from warm hotel golds into sickly greens/grays as dread rises; The Mimic (Nightmare chapters) uses near-monochrome cold desaturation; Pressure runs a teal-and-amber cold industrial grade; Apeirophobia's yellow-wallpaper liminal palette is a deliberate sickly single-hue.
    MOBILE: Pure Color3 values, zero memory cost. IMPORTANT mobile correction: Roblox renders noticeably darker on phones, so keep wall albedo Value at ~0.30-0.42 (not crushed to 0.10-0.15) — let the DARKNESS come from Lighting, keep the ALBEDO navigable so a bright phone screen in daylight can still read the geometry.
  * MaterialVariants with tiling PBR — non-repeating grounded concrete from a tiny texture library
    API: MaterialService.UseMaterialVariants, MaterialVariant (BaseMaterial, ColorMap, NormalMap, MetalnessMap, RoughnessMap, StudsPerTile), BasePart.MaterialVariant
    VALUES: In MaterialService insert a MaterialVariant: Name 'Concrete_Wall_MV', BaseMaterial=Concrete. Supply ColorMap + NormalMap + RoughnessMap (MetalnessMap near-black for non-metal). Concrete roughness map value ~0.80-0.92; wet-floor variant ~0.35-0.50 + Part.Reflectance 0.05. Set StudsPerTile to 6-8 so the pattern does not obviously repeat at human scale (4 = crisp but visibly tiling, 8 = softer, hides repeat). Apply via Part.MaterialVariant='Concrete_Wall_MV'. Keep the whole game to a LIBRARY of ~4-6 variants (wall concrete, floor concrete, plaster, wood, metal, rust) reused everywhere — this is what DOORS-tier realism actually is, not unique textures.
    GAMES: This is the core of how Grace, The Mimic, and Pressure achieve mesh-grade surfaces on Roblox; DOORS uses tiling wallpaper/wood/marble variants reused across the whole hotel — the same 6 materials carry 100 rooms.
    MOBILE: Budget carefully: mobile texture memory spikes hard (imports can push 500MB->1.3GB on Android). Keep tiling maps at 512x512 (256x256 for minor variants), power-of-two, under 1MB compressed each, and cap the library at ~6 variants. Reusing 6 tiling variants across the whole level is dramatically cheaper than unique textures and is fully mobile-viable.
  * Break flat wall planes with trim, baseboard, reveals and panel lines
    API: BasePart (thin parts for trim), negative-space geometry / inset parts, BasePart.Size and CFrame for offsets; optionally Texture instance for faux panel lines
    VALUES: Baseboard: a 0.4-0.6 stud deep, 0.75-1.0 stud tall part running the wall base in the dark-wood/metal trim color. Chair rail at ~3 studs height (0.2 deep). Ceiling reveal/crown at top. Panel lines: 0.1-0.15 stud deep dark recesses (thin inset parts or a dark Texture line) every 4-8 studs to break long walls. Door frames: 0.3-0.5 stud proud casing around every opening — never a bare hole in a wall. Vary part sizes deliberately; avoid every wall segment being identical dimensions. Where part count matters, fake reveals/panel lines with a Texture instance (a dark line strip) instead of geometry.
    GAMES: DOORS door casings, baseboards, wainscoting and crown molding are doing enormous work — Retro Mode removing them is what makes it snap back to looking like a blockout; Pressure's industrial reveal panels, rivet lines and trim strips read the facility as engineered rather than extruded.
    MOBILE: Trim adds parts — keep visible-room part count roughly under ~1000 and prefer Texture-instance panel lines over geometry on long walls. Do NOT union trim into walls to save parts (unions often render/collide worse and can INCREASE cost); keep them as simple parts and stream via WorldModel/StreamingEnabled if the level is large.
  * Grime, stains, cracks and wear via reusable Decals/Textures — abandoned means dirty
    API: Decal (Face, Transparency, Color3, ZIndex), Texture (StudsPerTileU/V for tiling dirt), Decal.Transparency for subtlety
    VALUES: Build a kit of 3-4 grime decals (water stain streak, dark corner-dirt/mold blob, crack, scuff) with transparent PNG backgrounds. Reuse them everywhere, varying: rotate 0/90/180/270, scale, flip, and tint via Decal.Color3 to a near-black brown ~Color3.fromRGB(40,34,26) so one decal reads as many. Keep each Decal.Transparency ~0.3-0.6 so it sits UNDER the material grain, not as a sticker. Concentrate grime where it physically collects: floor-wall corners, under water pipes, around door handles, ceiling-leak streaks running DOWN. Add a faint tiling dirt Texture over large floors (StudsPerTile ~10-16, Transparency ~0.6) to kill uniformity. Non-repeating rule: never place the same decal at the same rotation twice in one sightline.
    GAMES: DOORS and Pressure both lean heavily on stain/rust/mold decals concentrated at corners and under fixtures; The Mimic uses blood/grime overlays to age otherwise clean geometry.
    MOBILE: Extremely cheap if you REUSE — a 4-decal kit at 256x256 tinted/rotated across the level costs almost nothing. The mobile killer is many UNIQUE full-res decals, so treat the grime kit as a shared library and lean on Color3 tint + rotation for variety rather than new uploads.
  * Oppressive proportion — drop the ceiling and tighten the room
    API: BasePart.Size / model scale for ceiling height and corridor width; pair with Lighting fixtures at low mount height
    VALUES: Character is ~5-6 studs tall. Standard Roblox rooms are often 15-20 studs tall — that is why blockouts feel like empty gymnasiums. Set corridor ceiling to 8-9 studs, room ceiling 9-11, and use 7-stud drop-ceiling PINCH points at doorways/transitions for tension. Corridor width 6-9 studs (tight enough that an entity fills it). Vary ceiling height between spaces so low areas feel crushing by contrast. Add ceiling structure — beams, ducts, drop-tile grid, hanging fixtures — 0.5-1 stud below the ceiling plane so the top of frame is busy and low, not blank.
    GAMES: DOORS corridors and the Mines are deliberately tight and low; Rooms/Apeirophobia use claustrophobic drop-ceiling liminal hallways; Pressure's sub-facility corridors are narrow enough that entities block the whole passage.
    MOBILE: Pure geometry sizing, zero memory cost, and it actively HELPS mobile — smaller enclosed rooms cut draw distance and part count in view, which improves phone frame rate versus large open blockout volumes.
  * Cheap kitbash set-dressing that implies lived-in-then-abandoned
    API: Simple part kitbashes / free Toolbox meshes (vetted), Model grouping, CollectionService tags for reuse, PBR SurfaceAppearance reserved for 2-3 hero props only
    VALUES: Dressing checklist per room: (1) one broken/tipped furniture piece (chair, desk, locker); (2) scattered small clutter — papers, a mug, a shoe — placed ASYMMETRICALLY and slightly rotated off-grid; (3) a wall fixture — pipe run, electrical conduit, vent, flickering fixture; (4) signage/notice decals (exit signs, warnings, water-stained posters); (5) one story implication — a knocked-over chair facing a door, a dropped flashlight, a half-packed box. Rotate props a few degrees off 90 so nothing is grid-perfect. Reserve SurfaceAppearance PBR for only the 2-3 hero props the player gets close to; everything else uses built-in materials. Kitbash props from 3-6 simple parts rather than importing unique meshes.
    GAMES: DOORS dresses every room with period furniture, rugs, paintings and clutter (Retro Mode stripping this is the clearest proof of how much dressing carries the look); Specter and The Intruder use domestic props for lived-in realism; Pressure uses industrial clutter (crates, barrels, tools) to sell a working-then-failed facility.
    MOBILE: Kitbash from parts and REUSE a small prop kit via CollectionService/PrefabModels rather than many unique meshes. Cap SurfaceAppearance to 2-3 hero props (it is heavier than built-in materials). Keep prop meshes low-poly and their textures at 256x256; a shared 10-prop kit dresses the whole level within mobile budget.

########## Feel / Juice — the visceral camera, screen, audio, and interaction layer that makes horror land on the player's body (not the lighting or geometry). ##########
SUMMARY: The single most important thing in this dimension is that the feel layer is a continuous nervous-system signal: every second, micro-motion (a breathing camera), a never-truly-silent room-tone bed, and surface-correct spatial footsteps must tell the player's body "you are inside a living body, in a real place, and you are not safe" — and then that signal must be violently withdrawn (the silence drop) at the exact instant of threat, because startle scales with CONTRAST against a baseline, not absolute loudness. Our current blockout fails this not because it is bright, but because it is perceptually STATIC and DEAD: a locked default camera reads as a screenshot/menu, total silence with no bed reads as "unfinished" (and has nothing to drop from), flat or absent footsteps scream "test level," and one uniform dry reverb makes every space sound like the same box. Brightness is only half the problem — the absence of living motion and sound is what the body files as "safe." The fix is almost entirely client-side and cheap (CameraOffset math, looping Sounds, SoundGroup volume tweens, ImageLabel overlays), and most of it survives — some of it thrives — on a mono phone speaker. The craft is restraint: sub-perceptual amounts, held quiet, spent in one contrast-maximizing moment.
PRIORITY: If a developer can only do three things right now, in this order: (1) Room-tone bed + the silence drop (techniques 5 and 7) — audio is the fastest, cheapest route to fear and the research is unanimous that DOORS/Pressure/Apeirophobia lean on it harder than visuals; lay a 3-layer bed mixed for the 1-5 kHz phone band, routed through a SoundGroup you can slam to zero, and the dead room instantly becomes an alive, dreadful one. (2) Dual-layer breathing + walk head-bob on Humanoid.CameraOffset (technique 1) — ~30 lines of sine/cosine math, free on mobile, and it kills the 'dead viewport / menu' read that is our biggest current failure; without an embodied camera nothing else can feel like it's happening TO the player. (3) Surface-based spatial footsteps with the occasional non-synced extra step (technique 6) — routed through that same SoundGroup, it plants a real physical body in the space AND drops the first seed of 'something else is here' with zero entities spawned. Do these three and the identical gray blockout will already read as a genuinely inhabited, unsafe place; FOV, shake, screen-stack, reverb-per-zone, and weighted doors are the second pass that turns 'unsafe place' into 'polished dread.'
PITFALLS:
  - Locked, perfectly-still default camera (no breathing, no bob) — the visual system reads it as a screenshot/menu, so nothing feels embodied and nothing can feel physically threatened. This is our loudest current tell.
  - Total silence with NO room-tone bed — the brain files dead-air as 'unfinished/safe,' and worse, real silence loses all power because there is no baseline to drop FROM. Silence only works as a subtraction.
  - Default or absent footsteps at flat 2D volume — the single biggest 'amateur test level' cue. No surface variation, no spatialization, no stride timing = no physical body in a physical space.
  - One global dry reverb (or none) so every room sounds like the same box — the ear never gets a sense of enclosure, scale, or material, so the blockout stays 'a gray room' instead of 'a place.'
  - Instant-click doors and the default blue ProximityPrompt — zero weight, zero commitment beat, and the blue UI instantly screams 'Roblox template,' breaking immersion in one tap.
  - Over-juicing: constant heavy shake, permanent thick grain, chromatic aberration cranked, bob amplitude too high — reads as nausea and 'asset-flip filter,' not dread. The craft is restraint; effects must be sub-perceptual until spent in one moment.
  - Mixing dread into the bass (40-120 Hz) and testing on headphones — it evaporates completely on the mono phone speaker ~70% of players use. Dread must live in the 1-5 kHz band and be doubled by visual siblings.
  - Leaving FOV static at 70 and ExposureCompensation at 0 with a flat bright frame — nothing tightens, nothing breathes, nothing tunnels; the player's body is never told to brace.
  - Testing only on PC in Studio — Roblox renders DARKER on phones (a real, widely-reported, unresolved issue), so a look that's moody on PC can ship pitch-black-unplayable on the actual phones players use. Every darkness/vignette value must be verified on a real mid-range phone.
TECHNIQUES:
  * Dual-layer living camera — idle breathing + velocity-keyed head-bob
    API: Humanoid.CameraOffset (Vector3), driven per-frame by RunService.RenderStepped or BindToRenderStep at Enum.RenderPriority.Camera.Value; reads Humanoid.MoveDirection.Magnitude / HumanoidRootPart.AssemblyLinearVelocity; math.sin/math.cos with os.clock()
    VALUES: IDLE breathe: Y = math.sin(clock*2*math.pi*0.22)*0.06 (0.22 Hz ~= 13 breaths/min, +/-0.06 stud) plus slow sway X = math.sin(clock*0.5)*0.02. WALK bob: bobFreq = 1.6*(speed/16); Y = math.abs(math.sin(t*bobFreq*2*math.pi))*0.12, X = math.cos(t*bobFreq*math.pi)*0.08 (figure-eight). Blend idle->walk by MoveDirection.Magnitude, and smooth with CameraOffset = current:Lerp(target,0.15) each frame. Keep total |Y| under ~0.15 stud or it nauseates.
    GAMES: DOORS, Pressure, and Apeirophobia all run a CameraOffset bob in first person; the sin/cos figure-eight keyed to clamped X/Z velocity is exactly the open-source 'Configurable Head Bobbing' community resource pattern.
    MOBILE: Pure math on one property — zero GPU, zero network, fine at 30fps on low-end phones. Reduce amplitude ~30% when UserInputService.TouchEnabled, because small phone screens amplify perceived motion sickness.
  * Trauma-based scare shake (additive, self-decaying)
    API: Additive term on Humanoid.CameraOffset (or a separate Camera.CFrame offset inside BindToRenderStep); math.noise (Perlin) for smooth jitter; a 'trauma' scalar 0-1 that decays each frame
    VALUES: On event: trauma = math.min(trauma + amount, 1) — small event +0.3, big scare +0.7, entity slam 1.0. Each frame: shake = trauma^2 (square so small trauma stays gentle); offset = maxTranslate*shake*(math.noise(clock*freq,seed)*2-1) with maxTranslate = 0.4 stud, freq = 18; add roll Camera.CFrame *= CFrame.Angles(0,0, math.rad(2)*shake*noiseZ). Decay trauma = math.max(trauma - dt*1.4, 0) (~0.7 s to zero).
    GAMES: DOORS kicks the camera on Rush/Ambush and on door slams; Pressure jolts on entity proximity. This is the community-standard CameraShaker module model.
    MOBILE: Math-only, free. Cap maxAngle lower on mobile — rotational shake reads as nausea faster on a handheld screen; favor translational shake plus one quick FOV punch over big rotation.
  * FOV as a tension dial (hold-breath tighten + scare punch)
    API: Camera.FieldOfView, animated with TweenService:Create (TweenInfo EasingStyle/Direction) or manual lerp; driven by state (hiding, entity-near, sprint, scare)
    VALUES: Baseline 70. Hiding / 'holding breath': tween to 62 over 0.8 s Enum.EasingStyle.Sine, Out (claustrophobic tunnel-in). Sprint/panic: 76 over 0.25 s. Scare: instant set to 80, then tween back to 70 over 0.5 s Enum.EasingStyle.Quart, Out. Keep the resting band 62-76; past ~85 it distorts and reads comedic.
    GAMES: Standard in Pressure/DOORS sprint and hide states; FOV tighten-on-hide is a staple of the hide-and-hold horror subgenre.
    MOBILE: Single property tween, free everywhere. Mobile aspect ratios already feel a touch wider, so don't tighten below ~62 or touch players can't navigate.
  * Screen-effect stack — vignette + heartbeat pulse + film grain + fake chromatic aberration
    API: A ScreenGui (IgnoreGuiInset = true, high DisplayOrder) holding stacked ImageLabels; TweenService for the pulse; RunService.RenderStepped to scroll grain via ImageRectOffset on a sprite sheet; two offset tinted vignette copies for pseudo chromatic aberration. (No native vignette/grain post-effect exists in Roblox.)
    VALUES: Vignette ImageLabel: UDim2.fromScale(1,1), radial transparent-center/black-edge PNG, ImageColor3 = Color3.new(0,0,0), resting ImageTransparency = 0.45. Heartbeat pulse (entity near/low health): TweenService loop transparency 0.45 -> 0.2 -> 0.45, 0.4 s each way, ~50-70 bpm accelerating as threat closes. Grain: tiling ImageLabel, ScaleType = Tile, ImageTransparency = 0.9, advance ImageRectOffset by a random 8-16 px per frame. Fake chromatic aberration: two vignette copies, one ImageColor3 = Color3.fromRGB(255,0,0) at +1.5 px X, one Color3.fromRGB(0,120,255) at -1.5 px X, both ImageTransparency ~0.85; widen offset to ~4 px only during a scare, then tween back.
    GAMES: Vignette-via-ImageLabel is the standard Roblox approach; VHS/grain/chromatic-aberration overlays are widespread in Apeirophobia-style and analog-horror Roblox titles.
    MOBILE: These are UI layers, not shaders — nearly free on mobile GPUs and immune to FIB downsampling. But because Roblox renders DARKER on phones, a 0.45 vignette can crush a phone screen to unreadable: detect TouchEnabled and drop resting vignette to ~0.3 and grain to ~0.94, keeping the darkest falloff at screen edges, not where thumbs rest.
  * Three-layer room-tone bed (the 'never truly silent' spine), mixed for phone speakers
    API: Looping Sound objects in a SoundGroup ('Ambience') under SoundService; EqualizerSoundEffect on that group; randomized one-shots via task.wait(Random); a distant directional layer parented to an Attachment/Part with RollOffMode
    VALUES: Layer 1 (bed/air): soft continuous hiss/hum, Volume 0.12, Looped true, 2D (parented to SoundService). Phone speakers reproduce almost nothing below ~800 Hz, so carry the audible spine in the 1-4 kHz 'air' band; on the group set EqualizerSoundEffect.LowGain = -6, MidGain = +3, HighGain = +2. Layer 2 (sparse punctuation): creaks/drips/distant knocks fired every Random(8,40) s at Volume 0.2-0.35, PlaybackSpeed Random 0.92-1.08. Layer 3 (distant directional): a Sound on a far Part, RollOffMode = Enum.RollOffMode.InverseTapered, RollOffMinDistance 10, RollOffMaxDistance 120, Volume 0.5 — a sound with a location the player can't reach.
    GAMES: Pressure is built on continuous layered facility ambience for sustained tension; Apeirophobia's design is literally 'silence punctuated by distant, unidentifiable sounds' — exactly this sparse-punctuation layer.
    MOBILE: Audio is client-cheap. The EQ + mid/high mixing IS the mobile fix — dread that lives at 40-120 Hz does not exist on a phone speaker, so carry the whole effect in the 1-5 kHz band and double it with the visual siblings (vignette pulse, grain) so mobile players still feel it.
  * Surface-based spatial footsteps with an occasional non-synced 'extra' step
    API: Per-Enum.Material footstep Sounds parented to an Attachment at foot height on HumanoidRootPart for true spatialization; triggered on stride via Humanoid.Running + a stride accumulator (or the walk animation); routed through an 'SFX' SoundGroup; Humanoid.FloorMaterial (or a downward raycast) to pick the material set; PlaybackSpeed randomization
    VALUES: FloorMaterial -> sound set (Concrete, Wood, Metal grate, Water). Stride interval = 0.5*(16/speed) s. Each step: PlaybackSpeed = Random(0.94,1.06), Volume 0.35, alternate two L/R samples so it isn't a metronome. Spatialize via the foot Attachment with RollOffMaxDistance 25. Dread hook: roughly every Random(60,120) s, low probability, play ONE extra footstep sample from an Attachment 6-10 studs BEHIND the player at Volume 0.25, NOT synced to their stride.
    GAMES: DOORS and Pressure both use material-based footstep sets and spatially-positioned entity footsteps; the phantom extra footstep is a classic stalk cue.
    MOBILE: Cheap. Footsteps sit in the 1-3 kHz band and survive phone speakers well — keep the extra-step sample bright (a heel click, not a low thud) so mobile players actually register the threat cue.
  * The audio drop — silence as the loudest weapon, via SoundGroup slam
    API: Master SoundGroup.Volume on the Ambience/SFX groups, tweened with TweenService; paired with the vignette/FOV so vision compensates for the missing audio
    VALUES: The move: 2-4 s before a scripted beat, tween the Ambience group Volume 1 -> 0.03 over 1.5 s Enum.EasingStyle.Sine, In (the room 'holds its breath'), leaving only the player's own footsteps/breath. Hold near-silence 2-4 s. Then SLAM: on the scare frame, set the stinger/entity Sound.Volume to full AND Ambience back to 1 on the same frame — contrast, not absolute loudness, is the spike. Optional: mute everything for a single frame right before the slam for a hard 'cut.'
    GAMES: Explicitly the DOORS/Apeirophobia principle — restraint and prolonged quiet so the contrast makes the encounter exponentially more terrifying; Apeirophobia's silence-then-distant-sound loop is this on a slow cycle.
    MOBILE: Volume tweens are free, and — unlike bass dread — silence reproduces PERFECTLY on any speaker. The drop lands identically on the worst phone, which makes it your single best mobile scare: you aren't fighting the speaker's frequency limits at all.
  * Zone-matched reverb (every space sounds like its own volume)
    API: SoundService.AmbientReverb (global quick win, Enum.ReverbType) for a baseline; per-zone ReverbSoundEffect inside a 'World'/'SFX' SoundGroup (DecayTime, WetLevel, DryLevel, Density, Diffusion), swapped by region triggers (ZonePlus / touch parts). Keep dialogue+UI in a separate DRY SoundGroup — AmbientReverb is global and will smear them.
    VALUES: Global baseline: SoundService.AmbientReverb = Enum.ReverbType.StoneRoom (or Hallway for corridors). Tight room: ReverbSoundEffect DecayTime 0.6, WetLevel -10, DryLevel 0, Density 1, Diffusion 1. Long concrete corridor: DecayTime 2.4, WetLevel -4. Cavernous void: DecayTime 4.0, WetLevel 0. Crossfade WetLevel/DecayTime over ~0.5 s on zone change so it doesn't pop.
    GAMES: StoneCorridor/Hallway presets are the classic dungeon footstep-ring; Pressure's enclosed-facility sound leans on tight reverb, and big-space tails sell scale in Rooms/Apeirophobia voids.
    MOBILE: ReverbSoundEffect is cheap DSP on mobile CPU, and reverb tails sit across the mid/high band so — unlike bass — they reproduce well on phone speakers, making reverb one of the cheapest ways to buy perceived realism on mobile. Don't stack many active reverbs; one per zone.
  * Weighted interaction — heavy doors and non-default prompts
    API: ProximityPrompt (Style = Enum.ProximityPromptStyle.Custom, HoldDuration, MaxActivationDistance, RequiresLineOfSight, GamepadKeyCode); door hinge via TweenService on a CFrame/HingeConstraint; a creak Sound whose PlaybackSpeed tracks the tween; SFX SoundGroup (reverb-routed); a small Humanoid.CameraOffset nudge
    VALUES: Prompt: HoldDuration = 0.6 (a beat of commitment, not instant), MaxActivationDistance = 8, RequiresLineOfSight = true, custom minimal white UI to kill the default blue Roblox look. Door open: tween 90 degrees over 1.1 s Enum.EasingStyle.Quint, Out (heavy: fast start, long slow settle) — never Linear. Creak Sound.Volume 0.4, start with the tween, low PlaybackSpeed 0.85. On latch/close: a 0.2 s tween into frame + a low 'thunk' one-shot + a 0.06 stud downward CameraOffset nudge that recovers in 0.15 s.
    GAMES: DOORS' whole identity is the weighted, audible door open with a beat of dread before each room; hold-prompts and custom prompt UI are near-universal in polished Roblox horror.
    MOBILE: ProximityPrompt has native touch (tap/hold) support — keep HoldDuration <= 0.7 s so it isn't tedious on a touchscreen, and size the custom prompt for a thumb (~44 px min target). Give the creak/thunk mid-band harmonics so the 'weight' survives phone speakers even though the low thunk fundamental won't.

########## Scene composition and horror level-art principles on Roblox — using space, light placement, and sightlines to manufacture dread on a blockout, with exact Lighting/effect/light-instance values a developer pastes in today. ##########
SUMMARY: The single most important thing: horror is a SUBTRACTIVE medium — darkness is the default state of the whole scene and light is the rare, motivated exception, arranged in discrete pools with dark gaps between them. Our build fails at the composition level for one reason: it is uniformly lit, so every surface reads at the same brightness and the player's eye has nothing to fixate on, no pool to walk toward, no black to fear. Even "gray blockout" is fine — DOORS and Rooms are gray/brown blockout-grade geometry; what makes them terrifying is that 60-70% of every frame is near-black and the remaining 30% is broken into small warm islands the player is forced to cross. The fix is not "add darker paint," it is a two-move relight: (1) kill the global fill (Ambient/OutdoorAmbient to near-black, Atmosphere to eat the far distance) so darkness becomes the canvas, then (2) re-introduce light only as sparse, spaced, motivated practical sources with narrow cones, warm in the "safe" lobby and cold/absent toward the annex. On mobile — where Roblox tone-maps and quantizes DARKER than PC — the darkness must be built with a deliberate non-zero readability floor (lifted via ExposureCompensation + a ColorCorrection Brightness floor + a faint colored Ambient), never with a pure-black Ambient, or 70% of our players walk into invisible walls.
PRIORITY: 1) Kill the global fill FIRST (Technology=Future, Ambient≈(6,7,12), OutdoorAmbient≈(4,5,9), and critically EnvironmentDiffuseScale/SpecularScale=0.15, ClockTime=0). Nothing else works until the space between lights is actually black — this single step is why the build 'looks light' and is a 20-minute change. 2) Re-light as spaced pools (narrow-Angle=35 downward SpotLights every ~30 studs with real Neon fixtures, 60-70% of floor left dark, one motivated bright terminus per sightline to lead the eye). This converts the now-black canvas into composed dread instead of an empty void. 3) Add Atmosphere (Density 0.4→0.65 lobby→annex, Offset 0.25, cold-gray Color) to eat corridor ends and create thresholds, AND set the mobile readability floor (ExposureCompensation +0.4 on touch, ColorCorrection Brightness ~0.04, tiny personal PointLight) in the same pass so the darkness ships navigable on the 70% who play on phones. Do all three today; warm/cold zoning and oppressive-ceiling framing are the immediate next layer once the scene reads dark.
PITFALLS:
  - Evenly-spaced or omni lights that make every pool touch its neighbor — this silently rebuilds a fully-lit room and is our current #1 problem; if you can see two light pools overlapping with no black gap, it is not horror lighting.
  - Leaving EnvironmentDiffuseScale/EnvironmentSpecularScale at the default 1 — the skybox then floods every surface with uniform ambient bounce and no amount of dark paint or low Ambient will make the scene read dark. This is the hidden reason a 'dark' build still 'looks light.'
  - Ambient set to pure (0,0,0) 'to be extra dark' — looks fine on the developer's PC in a dark room, ships as an unnavigable black screen for the 70% mobile audience. Darkness must have a designed non-zero floor.
  - Lighting from nothing — a pool of light with no visible Neon fixture/bulb/window as its motivated source reads as fake and gamey; every light needs an in-world emitter the player can see.
  - Wide default SpotLight Angle (90+) — floods walls and ceiling, destroying the shaft/cone shape; horror cones want Angle 25-40.
  - Uniform color everywhere (all warm or all one gray) — kills the warm-safe vs cold-unknown zoning that makes progression feel like descent; a single palette says 'nothing here matters.'
  - Bright, visible, evenly-lit ceilings — instantly signals 'safe bounded interior' and deflates dread; ceilings should fall into black.
  - Corridors where you can see the whole length — no fog to eat the far end means no threshold, no negative space, nothing for the imagination to fill; the end of every sightline should dissolve.
  - Over-using Shadows=true on many SpotLights — tanks mobile framerate; budget ~4-6 shadow-casters per frame and let fill lights go shadowless.
  - Tuning the whole level only on a PC monitor — mobile renders darker and most phones are used in bright rooms; untested-on-phone darkness is the most common way a Roblox horror build ships broken.
TECHNIQUES:
  * Kill the global fill first — build the near-black canvas so pools can exist
    API: Lighting.Technology = Enum.Technology.Future; Lighting.Ambient; Lighting.OutdoorAmbient; Lighting.Brightness; Lighting.EnvironmentDiffuseScale; Lighting.EnvironmentSpecularScale; Lighting.ClockTime; Lighting.GlobalShadows; Lighting.ShadowSoftness; Lighting.ExposureCompensation
    VALUES: Technology = Future (mandatory — only Future gives crisp per-light local shadows from SpotLight/PointLight; ShadowMap is being retired). Ambient = Color3.fromRGB(6, 7, 12) — near-black with a faint cold blue, NEVER (0,0,0). OutdoorAmbient = Color3.fromRGB(4, 5, 9). Brightness = 1 (this is the SUN's indoor contribution — keep it low; the annex can go to 0). EnvironmentDiffuseScale = 0.15 and EnvironmentSpecularScale = 0.15 (these two are the hidden culprit of 'still light' — at the default 1 the skybox floods every surface with uniform ambient bounce and erases your pools; drop them). ClockTime = 0, GeographicLatitude = 0 so no sun angle sneaks warm light through gaps. GlobalShadows = true. ShadowSoftness = 0.2 (sharp shadow edges read as harder, more real). ExposureCompensation = 0 on PC baseline (mobile floor handled in its own technique).
    GAMES: DOORS' hotel sits at near-black Ambient with all illumination coming from placed wall sconces; Rooms is almost pure-black global with a flashlight as the only source; Grace uses Future + very low EnvironmentDiffuseScale to get its cinematic contrast. All of them treat global lighting as ~zero and paint with local lights.
    MOBILE: Future runs on modern phones but low-end devices auto-drop to Voxel and lose local shadows — do not rely on shadow shape to sell a scene; sell it with the light pool itself. Keep Ambient at (6,7,12) not (0,0,0) precisely because mobile crushes blacks harder; a literal-zero Ambient is navigable on PC and a black screen on a phone.
  * Pools-of-light rhythm — sparse spacing, narrow cones, dark gaps the player must cross
    API: SpotLight (Angle, Range, Brightness, Color, Shadows, Face) and PointLight (Range, Brightness, Color, Shadows) parented to small emitter parts; part.Material = Enum.Material.Neon on the visible bulb/fixture; SurfaceLight for strip/panel sources
    VALUES: RHYTHM: one practical light roughly every 24-40 studs of corridor, so a ~30-stud gap of near-black sits between each pool — aim for 60-70% of floor area unlit. Downward corridor SpotLight: Face = Bottom, Angle = 35 (narrow cone, NOT the 90+ default flood), Range = 18, Brightness = 3, Color = Color3.fromRGB(255,236,205) in the lobby, Shadows = true. Pair each SpotLight with a tiny co-located PointLight Range = 10, Brightness = 0.5, same color, Shadows = false as soft falloff so the pool edge isn't a hard disc. The visible fixture part: 0.4-stud Neon part at the emitter so the source itself glows (never light from nothing). Wall sconce variant: PointLight Range = 12, Brightness = 0.7, Color = (255,202,156). Rule: Range should die BEFORE it reaches the next fixture — if two pools touch, you've made a lit room again; increase spacing or drop Range.
    GAMES: DOORS spaces warm sconces down each hall with dark stretches between and relies on the flashlight to bridge them; Rooms/Apeirophobia use single overhead sources with hard falloff so you see one pool at a time; Pressure uses sparse caged hazard lamps with long black gaps in the Blacksite corridors. None of them light a whole room — they light stations along a path.
    MOBILE: Cap TOTAL shadow-casting lights visible in one frame to ~4-6; each Shadows=true SpotLight is expensive on phones. Give only the hero light per pool Shadows=true and make the fill PointLight Shadows=false. Range 18 is deliberately generous so the pool is still findable after mobile crushes the falloff edge.
  * Atmosphere as the 'vanishing wall' — hide the far end and make the doorway a black threshold
    API: Atmosphere (Density, Offset, Color, Decay, Glare, Haze) parented to Lighting; optional DepthOfField (FarIntensity, FocusDistance, FarDistance)
    VALUES: Atmosphere.Density = 0.4 in the lobby, ramp to 0.55-0.65 toward the annex. Color = Color3.fromRGB(70,74,82) (cold gray — this is the color the distance dissolves INTO). Offset = 0.25 (pushes haze off the camera so near objects stay crisp and only distance is eaten). Haze = 2. Glare = 0.2. Decay = Color3.fromRGB(40,44,52). Optional DepthOfField: FarIntensity = 0.35, FarDistance = 55, FocusDistance = 8 to soften the far dark. Tune so a straight corridor's end fades to near-invisible around 40-60 studs — the player should never see the whole hall.
    GAMES: The Mimic leans hard on distance fog to hide corridor ends and cheap geometry; Pressure uses dense blue-gray atmosphere for its drowned-facility depth; DOORS uses lighter haze but still fades hall ends. Fog is universally how Roblox horror hides the far distance and cuts draw cost simultaneously.
    MOBILE: Atmosphere is cheap and renders consistently across devices — one of the few effects that behaves the same on phone and PC, so it's your most reliable dread tool on mobile. Keep Density <= 0.65; above that, mobile's darker tone-map plus fog can black out navigation. Use Offset >= 0.2 so the phone player's immediate footing stays readable.
  * Lead the eye — one motivated light at the sightline terminus, and silhouette by backlight
    API: SpotLight/PointLight placed at the visual endpoint of a sightline (doorway, stair top, end of hall); back-placed SpotLight behind a prop/opening to rim-light it; part.Material=Neon signage; Beam (optional) for a visible shaft
    VALUES: At the end of each corridor or above each of the 4 room doorways, place ONE motivated source the player walks toward: doorway backlight SpotLight Face=Front pointing back toward the player, Angle=45, Range=22, Brightness=2, Color cold = Color3.fromRGB(150,175,190) so the far light reads 'not-safe.' For silhouette framing, put a SpotLight/PointLight BEHIND a doorway or object relative to the camera so foreground geometry goes black-on-light — Range=16, Brightness=2.5. Keep exactly ONE bright terminus per sightline; if two competing bright points are visible, the eye has no path. Optional Beam through a doorway: Beam.Width0/Width1=0.5, Brightness set low, LightEmission=1, Color faint, to draw a visible 'go here' shaft in the haze.
    GAMES: DOORS frames the next door with light so you always know where to go while dreading it; Pressure uses distant emergency glows as beacons down black halls; classic Rooms puts the exit-light at the sightline end. Silhouette-in-doorway is the signature Mimic/Specter jump-scare framing.
    MOBILE: A single bright terminus survives mobile crushing better than many dim ones — concentrate luminance rather than spreading it. Backlight/silhouette relies on contrast not shadow-mapping, so it holds up even when a low-end phone drops to Voxel and loses local shadows.
  * Warm-safe vs cold-unknown zoning — the lobby is the only warmth, and the threshold is where it dies
    API: Per-zone light Color3 on SpotLight/PointLight; ColorCorrectionEffect (TintColor, Saturation, Contrast, Brightness) optionally swapped per-zone via client script on region enter; Atmosphere.Color per-zone
    VALUES: LOBBY (relative safety): light Color = Color3.fromRGB(255,236,205) (warm tungsten), ColorCorrection TintColor = (255,248,238), Saturation = -0.1, Contrast = 0.1. THE 4 ROOMS (escalating strangeness): shift each successive room's practical Color cooler — Room1 (255,224,190), Room2 (220,224,220), Room3 (170,195,210), Room4 (140,170,195). ANNEX (the cold unknown terminus): light Color = Color3.fromRGB(120,150,180) or kill practical lights entirely and let only fog-glow remain; ColorCorrection Saturation = -0.35, TintColor = (210,222,230), Contrast = 0.18, Atmosphere.Color = (60,66,78). The DOORWAY between warm and cold is the composition beat: place the last warm light ~4 studs before the threshold and the first cold light ~4 studs after, with a genuinely dark gap ON the threshold so you physically pass through black to change worlds.
    GAMES: Pressure's whole identity is cold blue-green 'deep and wrong' broken by rare warm hazard-orange; DOORS contrasts warm hotel lobby against colder, darker later rooms; Grace uses warm/cold zoning to mark safe rooms vs hostile ones. Temperature contrast is the most-used Roblox-horror mood lever.
    MOBILE: Color contrast reads BETTER than brightness contrast on phones (mobile crushes luminance but preserves hue), so leaning on warm-vs-cold instead of bright-vs-dim keeps zones legible when a phone flattens your values. Apply ColorCorrection per-zone on the client; it's cheap and consistent across devices.
  * Oppressive verticality — dark ceilings, low-mounted downlights, and compressed headroom
    API: SpotLight Face=Bottom (downlights only, never up-lit ceilings); geometry: lower ceiling parts; Atmosphere/Ambient leaving the ceiling plane unlit; SurfaceLight Face=Bottom for flush panels
    VALUES: Mount every corridor practical LOW — emitter part at ~6-7 studs height, not at the ceiling — and aim it DOWN (Face=Bottom, Angle=35) so the cone hits the floor and the ceiling stays black. NEVER put an up-facing or omni light that reveals the ceiling. Drop corridor ceiling height to ~9-11 studs (from the typical open blockout 15-20) and let the top ~3 studs read as pure unlit black. For rooms that should feel cavernous-wrong instead, invert: raise the ceiling out of light Range (Range <= 14 with a 22-stud ceiling) so the top vanishes into black void. Keep Brightness on the floor pool, luminance falling off before it climbs the walls.
    GAMES: Rooms and Apeirophobia use flush ceiling downlights over black-above corridors; Pressure's facility ceilings dissolve into dark to feel deep/submerged; DOORS keeps hall ceilings dim so the warm sconces are the only vertical information. Oppressive-ceiling framing is standard for the 'trapped indoors' subgenre.
    MOBILE: Down-facing lights concentrate the few luminance units you can afford onto the floor/path — exactly the surface a mobile player needs to navigate — so this technique IMPROVES mobile readability while increasing dread. Lower ceilings also shrink the lit surface area, cutting fill-rate cost on weak GPUs.
  * The mobile readability floor — deliberate non-zero darkness that still navigates on a bright phone
    API: Lighting.ExposureCompensation; ColorCorrectionEffect.Brightness/Contrast; a faint colored Lighting.Ambient; small always-on PointLight on the character; client-side quality check via UserInputService.TouchEnabled / Lighting to branch settings
    VALUES: Build a floor, don't rely on pure black. Keep Ambient >= (6,7,12) as the absolute darkness floor. On touch devices, bump ExposureCompensation from 0 (PC) to +0.35 to +0.6 and set a ColorCorrectionEffect.Brightness = 0.03-0.05 with Contrast = 0.1 so crushed blacks lift just off zero — enough to see a wall silhouette, not enough to kill dread. Branch at runtime: if UserInputService.TouchEnabled then ExposureCompensation += 0.4. Give the player a faint personal PointLight on the Character HumanoidRootPart: Range = 8, Brightness = 0.25, Color = (200,205,215), Shadows = false — an invisible 'eye-adaptation' bubble so they're never in literal 0,0,0. If a flashlight is added later, it replaces this crutch. Validate on an actual mid-range phone at Graphics Quality ~4, outdoors in daylight, not on a PC monitor in a dark room.
    GAMES: This is the failure mode behind countless DevForum 'my horror game is too dark on mobile' threads; the games that succeed on mobile (DOORS, Pressure at scale) keep a small ambient/personal-light floor and lean on fog + color + sparse pools rather than literal blackness. DOORS' flashlight is exactly this readability-floor crutch made diegetic.
    MOBILE: This technique IS the mobile-safety layer — it exists to make every other technique above survive a phone. Test target: on a phone at Quality 4 in a lit room, a player standing in a dark gap between two pools must still make out the corridor walls and the next doorway's edge; if they can't, raise ExposureCompensation by +0.1 increments, not Ambient, to avoid washing out the pools.
