# Rooms (Roblox, nicorocks5555, 2020) — minimalist ancestor of DOORS: minimum viable dread teardown

> T5 research dossier - teardown:Rooms - generated 2026-07-06. Every finding: mechanism -> causal chain -> verdict -> Threshold translation -> where it lands -> source.

## Summary

Rooms (May 2020, ~17.1M visits, abandoned by its creator in Sept 2020) is the game DOORS credits as inspiration, and it generates dread with almost nothing: roughly eight repeating office-room templates, TWO entities (A-60, A-200), one looping ambient drone, numbered doors, a flashlight, lockers, and no stated objective ("endless with no real goal besides survival" — creator-confirmed). Its fear system is a baseline-and-deviation machine: repetition, near-silence, and progressive darkness build a baseline so stable that any deviation — a distant static hiss, a 0.3-second distortion in the ambience — reads instantly as mortal information. Fans call it scarier than its polished successor precisely because it never spends a jump scare: DOORS habituates with a bestiary of scripted events, Rooms keeps you at permanent anticipation ("the game starts innocent and cute, but the further you go, the more you change your mind"). The single biggest lesson for Project 001: dread is cheaper than content — fear lives in the BASELINE, and threats are merely corruptions of it; minimum viable dread = one legible baseline + one corruptible information channel + one refuge with a sensory cost + one visible measure of depth. Its failures are equally instructive: single-channel audio telegraphs, RNG rooms with no valid response, and device-dependent mechanics are exactly the trust breaks our Constitution forbids.

## Findings

### 1. [ADAPT] Teaching system / Act I-II (Arrival, Discovery) across all archetypes

- **Mechanism:** Nomenclature-as-tutorial: the entity called A-60 first spawns when door A-060 opens, then has a chance on every door after; A-200 likewise at A-200. The door nameplate is simultaneously progress bar, score, and threat calendar — the game teaches its own bestiary through room numbers with zero tutorial text.
- **Causal chain:** Numbered doors -> player learns entity-name = door-number threshold -> every door from A-050 onward becomes a countdown -> anticipation dread self-generated by the player before any content is spent
- **Threshold translation:** The Threshold never explains, but the facility labels its own spaces for its own indifferent purposes (stencils, plates, tally marks that were never meant for the player). Players decode that the labeling correlates with what happens there. Teaching via environmental nomenclature = Discovery-act material for every archetype; refuse a literal gamey counter — the world doesn't count for you, it counts for itself.
- **Source:** https://r-rooms.fandom.com/wiki/A-60 ; https://r-rooms.fandom.com/wiki/Rooms

### 2. [ADAPT] The Violent Rhythm (panic -> understanding -> control), Escalation/Climax acts

- **Mechanism:** Distance-encoded approach audio: A-60 telegraphs with an LTE-interference static/buzz that gets louder as it nears, audible rooms in advance; its travel speed increases the deeper you are, so the decision window shrinks with depth. Wiki explicitly advises turning your head to stereo-localize it — with a headset. Survival = enter locker; if all living players are hidden, it halts 5 seconds and despawns.
- **Causal chain:** Faint static (uncertainty: 'did I hear that?') -> volume ramp converts distance into a legible decision timer -> player chooses locker vs push-forward under pressure -> first death teaches the one rule; later encounters resolve panic into control
- **Threshold translation:** This is the Violent Rhythm's telegraph grammar: loudness = distance, growth rate = urgency, and the window is always wide enough for an observant player (decision, never reflex). But we adapt to multi-channel: the same approach must exist in sound AND in light behavior AND in physical response of the room (dust, props trembling), because phone speakers and deaf players kill single-channel audio. The despawn-when-unobserved-and-hidden rule becomes a learnable law the world never breaks.
- **Source:** https://r-rooms.fandom.com/wiki/A-60

### 3. [ADOPT] The Hidden Presence (doubt -> dread -> revelation); audio system architecture

- **Mechanism:** Corruption-of-baseline telegraph (the crown jewel): the game's ENTIRE soundscape is one ambience — FNAF Sister Location's 'Watch your 6', 2.5 minutes slowed 70% into a ~7-minute loop. A-200's spawn telegraph is a 0.3-second DISTORTION of that loop, heard as a 'thud'. The threat never adds a sound; it damages the sound that was already there.
- **Causal chain:** Unbroken ambient loop trains the ear on 'normal' -> a 0.3s corruption registers half-consciously -> doubt ('did the sound just... bend?') -> dread while scanning for confirmation -> revelation and mastery once the pattern is learned
- **Threshold translation:** Directly implements our law that fear lives in absence and contradiction, and fits the NO MUSIC directive perfectly: every Threshold space has a learnable room tone (ventilation, hum, drip). The Hidden Presence never announces itself — it corrupts the baseline: a missing beat, a doubled echo, silence where the hum should be. Sound is information; here the INJURY to sound is the information.
- **Source:** https://r-rooms.fandom.com/wiki/A-200 ; https://r-rooms.fandom.com/wiki/Sound_Effects

### 4. [ADOPT] Hiding system (all archetypes), especially Violent Rhythm and Hidden Presence climaxes

- **Mechanism:** Refuge with a sensory cost: hiding in a locker plays a heartbeat sound that masks outside audio; A-200 only retreats (moving slowly backwards through rooms before despawning) while all players hide, and A-60 passes blind. Safety trades away your information channel, and the exit decision must be made on incomplete data.
- **Causal chain:** Enter locker -> world audio masked by your own heartbeat -> safety costs information -> deciding when to leave is itself a dread decision -> fear persists INSIDE the safe state
- **Threshold translation:** Hiding in The Threshold muffles the world and makes your own body the loudest thing in it. The exit decision is observation-driven (light under the door, the room tone returning to normal), never a memorized timer. Safety that costs perception keeps every second of an encounter a decision — survival stays observation + adaptation, zero reflex.
- **Source:** https://roblox.fandom.com/wiki/Player:Nicorocks5555/Rooms ; https://r-rooms.fandom.com/wiki/A-200

### 5. [ADOPT] Room/level generation system; Act I Arrival (establish baseline) and Act III Escalation (break it)

- **Mechanism:** Repetition as deviation-amplifier: the whole endless game is ~8 templates (Room1-Room5 plus Storage/Break/Box rooms) with FIXED locker counts (0-3) and FIXED battery spawn points per template. Players unconsciously memorize the grammar, so anything off-pattern screams.
- **Causal chain:** Identical templates repeat -> player builds an unconscious spatial model for free -> ANY deviation (a moved locker, an extra shadow, a room that should not connect) is instantly legible as threat -> paranoia generated with near-zero asset budget
- **Threshold translation:** The Threshold's impossible architecture only reads as impossible against an established normal. Build a small, strict room vocabulary per encounter, repeat it until the player trusts it, then break exactly ONE rule at the authored moment. This is how 'rooms shouldn't connect' becomes a legible violation instead of visual noise — and it is also our mobile performance budget: few templates, high reuse, all render budget on light and sound.
- **Source:** https://r-rooms.fandom.com/wiki/Room_Types

### 6. [ADAPT] Resource/tool system; Act III Escalation pacing

- **Mechanism:** Darkness ramp + flashlight economy as escalation curve: rooms get progressively darker until light is mandatory; batteries (1:25 each) spawn at fixed per-template spots; when a battery dies the next auto-loads but the flashlight TURNS OFF and must be manually re-lit — a designed micro-blackout every 85 seconds of use. The 10-Robux Gummy Flashlight trades batteries for noisy, click-spam shake-recharging that holds charge only 3-5 seconds.
- **Causal chain:** Deeper = darker = shrinking information radius -> dependence on a depletable tool -> every battery swap forces a beat of total darkness and a manual re-light -> rhythmic vulnerability pulses baked into the economy itself
- **Threshold translation:** Adopt 'the information radius shrinks as you go deeper' and 'your tool fails in authored pulses'; REFUSE absolute pitch-black (unreadable on mobile — see mobile notes) and refuse resource-starvation deaths. In The Threshold, degrade information through fog, sightlines, and geometry rather than pure luminance, keep a survivable ambient floor, and never schedule a tool-failure pulse during a moment with no valid response.
- **Source:** https://r-rooms.fandom.com/wiki/Batteries ; https://r-rooms.fandom.com/wiki/Flashlight ; https://r-rooms.fandom.com/wiki/Gummy_Flashlight

### 7. [ADAPT] Death/retry system; Act V Resolution

- **Mechanism:** Death becomes observation: dying turns you into a ghost that cannot open doors, hide, sprint, or use light — but moves fast and watches the living. No revives; the run is over, back to the lobby. Death converts the player from actor to audience.
- **Causal chain:** Death -> forced spectator perspective -> player SEES the rule that killed them play out on others (or replays it mentally in the space) -> retry is informed rather than resentful -> the retry loop itself teaches
- **Threshold translation:** Single-player translation: on death The Threshold does not acknowledge you — the camera lingers, indifferent, long enough to reveal the information you missed (the cue you didn't hear, the locker you ran past). Death's Resolution act is a trust-repair mechanism: every death must be reconstructible as 'the information existed and I missed it'. That is our 'losing because info wasn't available = bad design' law, enforced by the death screen itself.
- **Source:** https://roblox.fandom.com/wiki/Player:Nicorocks5555/Rooms ; https://roblox-rooms-doors.fandom.com/wiki/Ghosts

### 8. [ADAPT] Encounter macro-structure; Discovery and Escalation acts; community/retention layer

- **Mechanism:** The unanswerable number as curiosity engine: no goal, no ending, no explanation — just doors counting up forever (world-record runs pushed past room 4022; old rooms never despawn, so the game itself eventually buckles under lag). The question 'how deep does it go / what spawns after A-200?' cannot be answered in-game, so the community wiki and YouTube became the metagame.
- **Causal chain:** Unanswerable depth question -> rumor and milestone lore (A-100, A-200, A-1000) circulate outside the game -> players push PAST their own fear out of curiosity -> fear and curiosity held in permanent tension = the retention engine
- **Threshold translation:** Refuse endlessness — Project 001 is encounter-based with one authored climax per encounter. Adapt the shape: within each encounter, a visible 'deeper = worse' gradient the player can read (architecture degrading, tone thickening) drives Escalation, and each encounter's ONE core question should leave a residue that is only answerable by playing again or by talking to other players. Curiosity that survives Resolution is free marketing on a platform run by young streamers.
- **Source:** https://roblox.fandom.com/wiki/Player:Nicorocks5555/Rooms ; https://r-rooms.fandom.com/wiki/Rooms

### 9. [ADOPT] Whole prototype design philosophy; per-encounter threat budget

- **Mechanism:** Jump-scare abstinence with a two-entity economy — the fan-stated reason the ancestor outscares the successor. DOORS fields a whole bestiary of scripted scares and gets video essays titled 'Roblox DOORS isn't SCARY AT ALL'; Rooms fields two entities and near-total silence, and fan forums vote 'rooms because the game is scarier', explaining 'not because of A-60, not because of spooky Jumpscares — the game starts innocent and cute, but the further you go, the more you change your mind.'
- **Causal chain:** Scripted scare events habituate on repetition -> arousal spikes then discharges (refund on dread) -> Rooms never discharges: silence + anticipation keep arousal unresolved -> the player's imagination does the escalation for free
- **Threshold translation:** Direct validation of our Constitution's core bet. One presence fully learned beats ten monsters half-learned. For the prototype: keep the threat count per encounter at ONE, spend our budget on the baseline (room tone, repetition, light), and let the climax be the only discharge — describable in one sentence, unforgettable because everything before it refused to discharge.
- **Source:** https://roblox-rooms-doors.fandom.com/f/p/4400000000000061064 ; https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XbQYAf8eFyQ ; https://r-rooms.fandom.com/f (fan forum quote)

### 10. [ADOPT] Art direction / entity visual design, all archetypes (especially Silent Witness and Hidden Presence)

- **Mechanism:** Low-poly underspecification: A-60 is just six cycling red-tinted 2D faces (one lifted from the band Disturbed's mascot) with a red glow; A-200 is a wobbling scribble. Horror-craft commentary on Roblox liminal games nails why it works: 'your brain fills in the gaps... a low-poly figure is somehow worse than a fully rendered one, because you're doing half the horror work yourself.'
- **Causal chain:** Underspecified threat image -> brain completes it with personal worst-case detail -> each player is scared by their own imagination -> scales across millions of players AND across low-end devices for free
- **Threshold translation:** The Threshold's presences stay underspecified: silhouette, wrongness of motion, and audio injury — never a detailed monster model. Render budget goes to the ROOM, not the entity. Bonus: underspecification is also our mobile performance strategy — a 2D face at 50 studs/second terrified more players than any mesh DOORS ever shipped.
- **Source:** https://gamertagmythras.com/blog/roblox/best-roblox-horror-games ; https://r-rooms.fandom.com/wiki/A-60

### 11. [REFUSE] Audio/telegraph system architecture; engineering non-negotiables

- **Mechanism:** The single-dependency telegraph collapse: Roblox's March 2022 audio-privacy update privatized all audio over 6 seconds, silently deleting the 7-minute ambience — and with it A-200's ONLY telegraph (the 0.3s thud was a distortion OF that ambience). The game's deadliest entity became literally undetectable; dedicated players survived by opening the Roblox debug menu and watching the AUDIO GRAPH spike — hacking the information back in from outside the game.
- **Causal chain:** Survival cue exists in exactly one channel -> platform policy deletes that channel -> entity now kills with zero warning -> game silently transitions from fair to unfair with no design change -> only tool-assisted players retain trust
- **Threshold translation:** Named law for Project 001: every survival cue exists in at least TWO independent channels (sound + light, sound + geometry, absence + motion), and no load-bearing information may depend on a single asset or platform feature. Also a luau-architect mandate: audit every telegraph for single points of failure the way you'd audit code. The players-using-debug-menu story proves players will FIGHT to keep a fair game fair — honor that.
- **Source:** https://r-rooms.fandom.com/wiki/A-200 ; https://r-rooms.fandom.com/wiki/Sound_Effects ; https://gamertweak.com/why-roblox-remove-audios/

### 12. [REFUSE] Encounter/room generation constraints; encounter-design gate checklist

- **Mechanism:** RNG no-response rooms: several room templates ('Room2', 'Room4', 'Box Room') contain ZERO lockers and are explicitly documented by the community as 'lethal'; if A-60 or A-200 rolls while you are mid-crossing one, no valid response exists — you die to dice, not to a decision.
- **Causal chain:** Template roll + entity spawn roll align badly -> player has learned the rule (hear static -> locker) and executes it -> there is no locker -> death despite correct play -> trust in the learned rule is broken
- **Threshold translation:** Constitution violation, named precisely: 'losing because info wasn't available' has a twin — losing because no response was available. Threshold rule: every space contains at least one valid response to every threat that can legally occur there (hide spot, sightline, retreat path, or guaranteed pre-warning long enough to backtrack). The dice may decide WHEN the test comes, never WHETHER it is passable.
- **Source:** https://r-rooms.fandom.com/wiki/Room_Types ; https://roblox-rooms-doors.fandom.com/f/p/4400000000000059476

### 13. [ADAPT] The Violent Rhythm variant mechanics; The Silent Witness (being seen = being still under observation)

- **Mechanism:** DOORS' Rooms subfloor A-90 stillness check — the successor's one new idea: a quick knock, then a monochrome face with a red STOP sign; ANY movement including camera movement deals 90% damage; while A-90 shows, A-60/A-120's speed decreases to keep the combination survivable. Community verdict: memorable but a gotcha — 'extremely unpredictable, one wrong slip can kill you', and it punishes the instinct (look around) that the rest of the game trained.
- **Causal chain:** Knock (1-beat warning) -> stop sign (unambiguous demand) -> player must freeze -> camera movement also punished -> twitchy players and mobile players (touch-look) die to input noise, not to a decision
- **Threshold translation:** The GRAMMAR is gorgeous — a threat that demands stillness inverts every chase rule and creates panic->understanding->control in one beat, and the speed-compensation when threats overlap is real systems craft (adopt that). But we judge stillness on AVATAR movement only, with generous grace and a clearly learnable duration; punishing camera-look on a platform where 70% of players steer the camera with a thumb is a refused gotcha.
- **Source:** https://www.thegamer.com/roblox-doors-complete-rooms-guide/ ; https://doors-game.fandom.com/wiki/The_Rooms ; https://villains.fandom.com/wiki/A-90

### 14. [REFUSE] Difficulty/pacing doctrine; session structure for mobile

- **Mechanism:** Attrition-as-difficulty in the polished successor: DOORS' Rooms runs 2-5 hours, no revives, pitch black for hundreds of levels, A-120's telegraph is DELIBERATELY quieter than A-60's ('considerably harder to pick up'), and the community essay's core complaint is that after all entities are active 'you still have roughly an hour and 45 minutes... allowed to make one mistake once.'
- **Causal chain:** Difficulty raised by lengthening the run and attenuating information -> late deaths destroy hours -> fear is replaced by anxiety about wasted time -> dread curdles into resentment
- **Threshold translation:** Two named anti-patterns for Project 001: (1) never scale difficulty by making information QUIETER — scale it by making decisions RICHER; (2) session length is a mobile law — encounters resolve in short sessions (5-15 min), and nothing punishes a player with hours of lost progress. Dread must be per-moment, not per-investment.
- **Source:** https://jtoh.miraheze.org/wiki/User_blog:NoobIsReallyCool/video_essay-ish_thing_i_wrote_about_the_rooms ; https://www.thegamer.com/roblox-doors-complete-rooms-guide/

### 15. [REFUSE] Engineering (luau-architect) non-negotiables; QA checklist

- **Mechanism:** Device-dependent mechanics: the sprint bar depletes at a rate tied to the player's FRAMERATE, and the Gummy Flashlight's 3-5 second charge 'varies by players' FPS'; batteries even drain while the game is paused. The same inputs produce different survival odds on different hardware.
- **Causal chain:** Low-end device -> different frame timing -> sprint/charge behave worse -> the exact player base most likely to be on cheap phones gets a harder game -> deaths that are hardware verdicts, not decisions
- **Threshold translation:** Server-authoritative from day one is already our non-negotiable; this finding sharpens it: every timer, drain rate, and telegraph window lives on the server clock, never in frame deltas. Test every encounter at 20 FPS on a throttled device before calling it done — the baffled bar includes the worst phone in the room.
- **Source:** https://roblox.fandom.com/wiki/Player:Nicorocks5555/Rooms ; https://r-rooms.fandom.com/wiki/Gummy_Flashlight ; https://r-rooms.fandom.com/wiki/Batteries

## Trust breaks (their haters are our free QA)

- Lockerless 'lethal' room templates + entity spawn RNG = deaths where the learned rule (hear static -> hide) has no executable answer; correct play still dies to dice.
- The March 2022 Roblox audio-privacy update silently deleted the ambience and with it A-200's only telegraph — the game's deadliest entity became undetectable overnight; survivors resorted to watching the Roblox debug menu's audio graph to see the spike A-200's sound would have made. A single-channel telegraph is a single point of failure.
- Framerate-dependent mechanics: sprint bar depletion and Gummy Flashlight charge duration vary with the player's FPS — hardware decides survival, hitting low-end (mobile) players hardest.
- Batteries drain while the game is paused — the resource clock ignores the player's consent to be in the game.
- Audio-only telegraphs exclude deaf/hard-of-hearing players and anyone on phone speakers; the community itself diagnosed this — fan remakes shipped an explicit 'Deaf Mode' (visual cues replacing sound) as an apology patch, and the wiki's A-60 advice literally requires a headset for stereo localization.
- In the DOORS successor floor: 2-5 hour runs with no revives where one late mistake erases everything ('allowed to make one mistake once' — community essay); A-90 damaging players for CAMERA movement punishes the look-around instinct the game itself trained; A-120's telegraph being deliberately quieter than A-60's is difficulty-by-information-starvation.
- Endless depth with no room cleanup: old rooms never despawn, so deep runs (A-1000+) accumulate crippling lag — performance itself becomes an unannounced difficulty modifier and run-killer.

## Mobile notes

Rooms' fear core is close to maximally mobile-hostile, which is exactly why the extraction matters. What dies on a phone: (1) absolute pitch-black — Roblox DevForum threads document that identical scenes render DARKER on mobile than PC, and small glossy screens played in daylight turn 'atmospheric darkness' into 'literally nothing visible'; players respond by maxing brightness or quitting, both of which delete the design; (2) stereo localization — the A-60 wiki's own survival advice ('turn sideways to tell which ear the static is in') requires a headset; phone speakers are mono-ish, so distance-encoded audio survives but DIRECTION-encoded audio does not; (3) shake-to-recharge becomes tap-spam, and FPS-tied drain rates punish the cheapest devices hardest; (4) 2-5 hour attrition runs collide with short mobile sessions. What SURVIVES on a small bright screen with a phone speaker: the numbered door (visual, high-contrast, load-bearing), the repeated room grammar and its violations (geometry reads at any brightness), the corrupted-baseline telegraph IF mirrored visually (light flicker, prop tremble — DOORS already learned this: its Rooms version added a flashlight-flicker that accompanies A-60's static), the locker decision, and volume-ramp-as-timer (loudness works on any speaker; direction doesn't). Threshold rules derived: darkness is relative never absolute (shrink the information radius with fog/sightlines/geometry, keep an ambient floor readable on a TN phone panel at 50% brightness); every audio cue has a visual twin; timing server-side; encounters resolve in 5-15 minutes.

## The surprise

The entire horror system had one load-bearing wall, and it was a borrowed audio file: the game's whole soundscape was a single FNAF: Sister Location ambience ('Watch your 6') slowed 70% into a 7-minute loop, and the scariest telegraph in the game — A-200's arrival — was nothing but a 0.3-second DISTORTION of that loop. The most feared sound in a game played 17 million times was a compression artifact of someone else's asset. When Roblox's 2022 audio policy privatized files over 6 seconds, that one deletion simultaneously destroyed the atmosphere AND turned the deadliest entity invisible — and the community's response was the real surprise: players kept playing the broken, abandoned game by opening Roblox's developer debug menu and watching the live audio graph for the spike A-200's silenced sound still produced. Players will build prosthetic telegraphs out of engine internals to keep a fair game fair. That is how much trust a two-entity game with eight room templates had earned.
