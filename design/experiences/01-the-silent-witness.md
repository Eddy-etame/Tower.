# EXPERIENCE: The Silent Witness

**Archetype:** The Silent Witness — paranoia / violation (being watched, recorded, studied)
**Designer:** Eddy + Claude · **Date:** 2026-07-06 · **Status:** Stage 2 draft — Gate B verification in progress (Gate A APPROVED by Eddy, 2026-07-06)
**Target playtest utterance (pass criterion):** *"it's been watching me this whole time?"* — unprompted, at least once per playtest.

---

## STAGE 1 — THE WHITEBOARD

**Inspiration** — The Closing Shift's retroactive photo-wall reveal, ECHO's replay-of-your-own-conduct, Anatomy's house-that-gatekeeps, P.T.'s corrupted footstep echo — extracted, not copied: their stalkers are obsessed with you; The Threshold's watcher is **indifferent**. You are not loved or hated. You are *specimen data*. (Research: [craft-silent-witness.md](../research/craft-silent-witness.md) findings 1, 8, 9, 10.)

**Emotion** — **Violation.** Arc: unease (am I alone?) → confirmation (I am not) → violation (I never was, and it wrote everything down).

**Hook** — Every room the player enters produces one dry mechanical *click* — small, sourceless, consistent. Nothing follows. By the third room the player has a theory; by the fifth, a ritual: enter, freeze, listen. The first room that **doesn't** click is scarier than any that did. (Sound kit: craft study finding 13.)

**Memorable Moment** — The player finds the annex behind the walls: a filing room. Hundreds of dossiers. One is open on the desk — **theirs**. Their route through the previous rooms marked on a floor plan. Tally marks matching their actual door entries. Their pauses, timed. And the last page: a sketch of them in their current idle pose — **mid-stroke, unfinished**. Whatever draws was here a moment ago. Or still is. Every mark is forensically true because the server logged the real session — when a streamer checks the tallies against their VOD, they match. That is the "THIS AIN'T REAL" beat: the game proves it was watching.

**Experience in one sentence** — *You realize the place has been taking notes on you — and you find the notebook.*

**Replayability** — The dossier is generated from the live session log, so it is **never the same document twice**: crawl the whole run and the notes say so; sprint and they say that instead. Players replay to test what it records ("does it count how long I stared at the painting?") — theory-testing is the replay engine, and the community comparison of dossiers is the conversation engine.

**Multiplayer** — *What changes when a second human is present?* The dossiers cross-reference: "Subject B waits for Subject A before every door." The violation doubles — it studied your **friendship**. (Prototype: single-player; field carried per audit F13.)

**Runtime Test** — Deserves to exist because no Roblox horror game has ever shown the player verifiable evidence of their own session as the scare. Observation horror is the platform's rising paradigm (front-page teardown, 2026-07) — this is its endgame: the player *is* the content.

### The Five Questions
1. **What inspired this?** Watched-horror canon (P.T., Closing Shift, Anatomy, ECHO) transformed through indifference — the watcher takes notes the way a researcher does, not the way a stalker does.
2. **What emotion?** Paranoia resolving into violation.
3. **What question is the player answering?** **"Do I let it see me?"** — moving fast feeds it motion; standing still feeds it detail; every behavior is authored testimony. (No other encounter asks this.)
4. **What is the unforgettable moment?** Opening your own unfinished dossier.
5. **Why will players remember this?** Because the evidence was *really theirs* — the game replays your own conduct back at you, and that violation survives after quitting.

### Runtime Test checks
Curiosity: the click ritual begs investigation ✓ · Fun: detective-work against the room itself ✓ · Memory: your own dossier ✓ · Conversation: "check the tallies against my VOD" ✓ · Originality: no stalker, no entity, no photos of a victim — clinical specimen files ✓ · Purpose: proves the studio thesis "the room is an extension of the monster" with zero monsters ✓

### Mechanical spine (for Gate A honesty — full design is Stage 2)
Baseline-then-deviation rooms (P.T.-loop literacy; Exit 8 categorical-change fairness: silhouette-scale changes only, always a second corroborating trace) · unobserved-reorientation tells (the chair now faces the door you came in through — never seen moving, always seen *having* moved) · server session log powering the dossier (ECHO device: playback of a real log, no AI) · death/failure text as the place's clinical observation log ("Subject held the handle 3.1 seconds. Subject did not look up."). All server-authoritative; the dossier references **only this session's in-game behavior** — never account data, never chat. *(Panel edit 2026-07-07: the late footstep echo was removed — reserved for The Hidden Presence.)*

## STAGE 2 — THE DESIGN (Constitution climax-first order)

### 1. Climax Moment
**The player opens their own dossier and the sketch of them — in their current idle pose — is unfinished, mid-stroke.**

### 2. World-Changing Lesson
**Nothing you do here goes unseen.** (Exactly one thing; it permanently reframes every later encounter — the player now behaves as testimony.)

### 3. Core Question
**"Do I let it see me?"** — every behavior is authored data: move fast and it learns your motion; move deliberately and you control what it learns. Unique across the four docs.

### 4. The Threat & Threat Ambiguity
The player arrives assuming a genre stalker — an entity to spot, flee, or outwit. There is no entity. The threat is **the player's own assumption plus the environment itself**: the place is the watcher, and the danger is never to their body but to their privacy of self. The reveal is inevitable in hindsight: every click, every reoriented chair pointed at THEM the whole time. One hard rule discovered late: the Witness tolerates being *known* — it does not tolerate being *edited* (see Failure).

### 5. Discovery Design
Choreographed per horror-craft (evidence before entity; there is no entity):
- **Act I teaches a baseline** (P.T.-loop literacy): a short corridor loop presents the same room twice before it ever deviates — the player must trust a room completely before the room may deviate. Impossibility lives in connections, never inside a room's stable interior (fairness contract).
- **Discovery is categorical, never pixel-level** (Exit 8 contract): object moved / reoriented / added / light changed — at silhouette scale, always while unobserved, always with a **second corroborating trace** (drag-path in dust, a click heard minutes earlier that only now makes sense).
- **The tells converge on one meaning:** changes always orient toward the player — the chair faces the door they entered through; the painting's gaze points at their entry point on every re-entry (never seen moving; always seen *having* moved).

### 6. The Five Acts (progressively shorter)
- **Act I — Arrival:** the click ritual (one dry mechanical click per room entered — 1:1, learnable) **with its real-time visible twin: a fresh tick appears on each room's doorframe as the player enters** (the muted player's hook, and it pre-teaches tally = entry so the Act IV dossier becomes child-verifiable evidence); the baseline loop; nothing threatens. **The first deviation is personal, not generic: the first changed object already faces the player's entry door, its drag-trace pointing at THEM** — the toward-you signature is in the first clip, not the fourth minute. ~4 min.
- **Act II — Discovery:** more unobserved reorientations + second traces; the player proves it to themselves (leave, return, compare). The realization: not "something is in the room" — "the room is studying *me*." ~3 min.
- **Act III — Escalation:** the watching gains capabilities, **each strictly traceable to this session's logged behavior** (the one escalation players certify as fair — Alien: Isolation reception study): their repeated route pre-opened; a chalk mark and seconds-tally at the exact spot they stood longest; a pencil-scratch behind the wall keeps pace with them and pauses when they pause — **and the scratch is loudest at one wall of the un-clicking room** (see §8: the silent room is the tell that points at the annex). ~2.5 min.
- **Act IV — Climax:** the annex — a filing room that is bigger than the wall it hides behind. Hundreds of identical dossiers. Theirs is open: their route inked on a floor plan **with numbered entry arrows, tallies grouped per door** (the artifact is self-verifying inside a single clip), their pauses timed, and the unfinished sketch. **The money frame is staged: a dark glass pane behind the desk co-frames the player's avatar and the sketch in one shot, the pencil beside it still rocking** — something was JUST here. Clinical. Filed. **Subject, not beloved.** ~90 sec.
- **Act V — Resolution:** the exit door beyond the annex already stands open — it knew they'd finish. As they cross out: one soft scratch behind them — a final tally. Done. ~20 sec.

### 7. Choice Points (2–3, each viable but risky)
1. **How to be seen:** move loud and fast vs. slow and deliberate — **and what it learned determines which Act III capabilities manifest**: fast players get their route pre-opened and paced; still players get the breath and the chalk. Same fairness law, genuinely different lived encounter.
2. **The wall:** the pencil-scratch — linger and learn its rhythm (it pauses with you: it reacts) vs. strike the wall (provocation — warned once, see Failure) vs. walk on (the not-knowing compounds).
3. **The dossier:** **entering the annex is the exit condition; reading is optional.** Read it fully (deepest violation) vs. leave without reading (carrying unconfirmed dread out) vs. destroy it (provocation → the fail state — and some players will choose it on purpose).

### 8. Information Map (Trust — all of it exists in-world)
Click = entry logged (1:1, testable) **with its visible twin: the doorframe tick, same beat** · reorientation = always toward you, only unobserved, always second-traced · scratch-pause = it reacts to your stillness · pre-opened doors = your own repeated route (self-traceable) · chalk mark + seconds-tally = the spot you stood longest (self-traceable) · **the silent room = the annex-pointer**: one room never clicks and never gains a doorframe tick, because the note-taker is on the other side of its wall — the scratch is loudest there, and the dossier's floor plan marks that room differently (the taught 1:1 rule never lies: the relay is the note-taker, and here you are AT the source) · **THE WARNING (unique event, never a silence):** on first destructive input, every dossier in view snaps shut at once, the sketch's pencil-line visibly freezes, and one sharp pencil SNAP (>1kHz) fires — the only near-sting in the encounter, the place breaking its own composure. Multichannel, mute-proof, unmaskable · dossier contents = the actual session log. Hard bans (empirical trust law): no psychic reveals, no teleported evidence, nothing untraceable.

### 9. Failure Design
The only failure is **editing the record** (tearing the dossier, striking the scratch-wall) — and it is a **two-stage input contract, explicit on touch**: the first tap/strike triggers the warning (dossiers snap shut + pencil-line freeze + SNAP) and commits nothing; only a second, separate, held input performs the edit. Punishment: lights drop for a beat and the player is **re-filed — but a re-filed specimen is already documented: the place fast-tracks it.** They wake in the annex antechamber, doors along their logged route standing open, baseline loop skipped — back at the dossier in under 90 seconds, and it now contains the attempt: *failure literally becomes content.* Players who fail immediately want to see what it wrote about them — and can, inside the session budget.

### 10. Sound Design (no music — SFX / ambience / silence only)
The kit (all >1kHz, mono-safe, phone-speaker-proven): the dry entry click (twinned by the doorframe tick) · the pencil-scratch pacing them · a breath that is not theirs, only when stationary · the warning SNAP (once, only ever as the warning). Silence deployed once, **with an authored meaning that keeps the click contract honest:** the one room that doesn't click is the room the annex hides behind — the absence is a pointer, not a lie (§8). *The late footstep echo was cut from this encounter — corrupted body-audio is reserved exclusively for The Hidden Presence's climax (cross-doc uniqueness, Gate B panel).* Every audio tell has a visible sibling in the same beat — sound is a channel for the information, never its container.

### 11. The Space
Baseline rooms austere, learnable, high-contrast (small vocabulary of watchable objects at silhouette scale). The annex violates only *capacity* — bigger than its wall — because the Witness's impossibility should be architectural, not decorative. The room is the extension of the monster; here, literally: the walls take notes.

### 12. Mobile Sanity Notes
Categorical silhouette-scale tells (Exit 8's vocabulary is mobile-native) · dossier reads visually — floor plan, tallies, sketch; **no prose walls** (young players don't read) · clicks/scratch live mid/high band (phone speaker physics) · no gaze-precision mechanics — all observation is room-coarse.

### 13. The Cut List
**Cut:** any visible entity or silhouette for the watcher (imagination outperforms mesh; embodiment kills indifference) · cameras/monitors motif (reads modern-security; handwritten records are timeless and worse) · the player's username in the dossier (they are "Subject" plus a number — indifference law and platform safety both) · any audio captured from the player's real mic (never — privacy, and impossible by design: dossier renders only abstract in-session marks, no player-authored content) · a second annex (one revelation, undiluted).
