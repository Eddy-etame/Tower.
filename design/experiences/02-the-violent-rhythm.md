# EXPERIENCE: The Violent Rhythm

**Archetype:** The Violent Rhythm — panic → understanding → control
**Designer:** Eddy + Claude · **Date:** 2026-07-06 · **Status:** Stage 2 draft — Gate B verification in progress (Gate A APPROVED by Eddy, 2026-07-06)
**Target playtest utterance (pass criterion):** *"wait — I know when it comes now."* — unprompted, at least once per playtest.

---

## STAGE 1 — THE WHITEBOARD

**Inspiration** — INSIDE's fixed-period shockwave (the final crossing too long to abort), Pac-Man's universal phase tell, ECHO's world-that-updates-on-its-own-schedule, Alien: Isolation's forgiven determinism vs. resented randomness — extracted through the Threshold lens: **the rhythm is the place's own metabolism.** It isn't hunting you. It's *breathing*, and you are standing in its airway. (Research: [craft-violent-rhythm.md](../research/craft-violent-rhythm.md) findings 3, 4, 12; industry's key empirical law: **determinism is forgiven, randomness is resented** — the scariest thing our threat can be is PUNCTUAL.)

**Emotion** — **Panic that becomes mastery.** The genre's most famous "failure" — dread collapsing into "a tactical and precise decision" (Thomas Grip on Alien: Isolation) — is this encounter's *designed payoff*. We don't prevent the flip; we author the exact moment it happens.

**Hook** — The player enters a long machine gallery mid-cycle: lights surge, a wall of violence crosses the space on a fixed period — and takes something visible with it (a door ripped shut, debris swept the corridor's length). First exposure is **witnessed from total safety** (rule of threes: witness → rehearse → test). The player's first thought is *"I cannot cross that."* The encounter exists to make that sentence false.

**Memorable Moment** — Act IV: a crossing too long to abort, no cover, and the tell fires mid-way. Every instinct says run. The learned rule says the opposite: **stop on the scorched beat-mark and stand still while it passes around you.** The player walks into the open *as it comes* — and it passes without touching them, because they finally speak its time signature. The clip is a player standing motionless inside the event that killed them in Act II.

**Experience in one sentence** — *You stop running from the thing on the schedule and walk through it on the beat.*

**Replayability** — Mastery replay: decode faster, cross earlier, take the tighter line. 2–3 valid approaches (ride the beat in the open · chain the short covers · spend the room's own machinery to stall one cycle) mean runs LOOK different; the "what if I'd crossed on the half-beat?" question is built into the schedule itself.

**Multiplayer** — *What changes when a second human is present?* The rhythm becomes a shared language — both cross on one beat or split across two, and "GO — NOW" shouted at the right beat is co-op's purest trust exercise. (Prototype: single-player; field carried per audit F13.)

**Runtime Test** — Deserves to exist because Roblox horror teaches *hide from fast things* (DOORS' grammar) and never teaches *walk through the fast thing because you understand it*. Precision on the platform claim (panel correction, citing [teardown-rooms.md](../research/teardown-rooms.md)): Rooms' A-90 punishes movement during a sub-second reactive gotcha — a reflex test; ours is a **scheduled, pre-marked, rehearsed, walk-then-stand decision with zero reaction window**. The *mastery* arc — panic converted to deliberate control — remains unclaimed.

### The Five Questions
1. **What inspired this?** Fixed-period lethality done honestly (INSIDE), phase tells done universally (Pac-Man), schedules that belong to the world, not the player (ECHO).
2. **What emotion?** Panic transmuted into control — fear's opposite, earned inside one room.
3. **What question is the player answering?** **"When do I move?"** — never *whether*, never *how fast*: **when**. (No other encounter asks this.)
4. **What is the unforgettable moment?** Standing still inside the passing violence, on purpose, and living.
5. **Why will players remember this?** Because their own body learned the beat — the knowledge is physical, and the final crossing is the bravest thing the prototype asks anyone to do.

### Runtime Test checks
Curiosity: what IS the cycle, and why does the room have beat-marks? ✓ · Fun: pattern-decoding with a walkable victory lap ✓ · Memory: the stand-still ✓ · Conversation: "I stood IN it" ✓ · Originality: threat-as-metabolism, no chase ✓ · Purpose: proves observation-beats-reflexes as a thesis — a clever slow player wins this encounter *by design* ✓

### Mechanical spine (for Gate A honesty — full design is Stage 2)
Fixed period, **bus-timetable law**: missing a window costs one cycle of waiting, never a life · every phase boundary broadcast by ONE universal whole-room tell (machinery state + light cadence — mobile-proof, mono-proof, per Pac-Man/DOORS evidence) · rule of threes enforced: witness safely (Act I), rehearse with abort-friendly cover (Act II), one authored single-parameter mutation (Act III — the cycle gains a half-beat stutter; old rule still true, new rule stacks) · climax crossing (Act IV) walkable by the slowest player · timing precise, position vague (RE2 mixing law) · schedule server-side; the "entity" does not exist between events (archive law) · **REFUSED:** any reflex minigame (DOORS Heartbeat QTE is the named anti-pattern), any randomized schedule, any unannounced rule change mid-crisis.

## STAGE 2 — THE DESIGN (Constitution climax-first order)

### 1. Climax Moment
**Caught mid-crossing with no cover, the player stops on the scorched beat-mark and stands still while the surge passes around them.**

### 2. World-Changing Lesson
**This place has a pulse — and anything that repeats can be learned.** (One thing; it permanently converts every later rhythm, cadence, and cycle in the game into readable information.)

### 3. Core Question
**"When do I move?"** — never *whether*, never *how fast*. Unique across the four docs.

### 4. The Threat & Threat Ambiguity
The visible threat: a wall of violent pressure — the gallery's **exhale** — crossing the space on a fixed period, telegraphed and lethal. The ambiguity is the player's own assumption: *speed saves*. It does not. Sprinting is how this encounter kills you; survival is position + timing, and the fastest player and the slowest player have identical odds. The reveal (the beat-marks work; running doesn't) is inevitable in hindsight: the debris told them from the first minute — everything loose piles up in the lee spots, and nothing survives in the open lanes.

### 5. Discovery Design
Consequence-first (rule of threes, enforced as law):
- **Witness (zero risk):** from the entry mezzanine, the player watches one full cycle take something visible — a hanging cart torn down the gallery's length — **while, in the same shot, a crate resting on a scorched beat-mark survives untouched, both cycles.** The period, the tell, the price, AND the sanctuary are all demonstrated before the player can be touched (rule of threes applies to the mark itself, not just the cycle).
- **The universal tell** (Pac-Man law — one unmissable boundary signal): wall lamps brighten and die **in sequence toward the surge's origin** — direction and countdown in a single whole-room event. Its audio twin: the gallery's rising mechanical inhale. Loudness IS the countdown.
- **Environmental information:** scorch-lanes vs. clean floor inside the lee of structural ribs; debris accumulation marks every safe spot. The room's history teaches the room's rule.

### 6. The Five Acts (progressively shorter)
- **Act I — Arrival:** mezzanine; two full cycles witnessed; the cart destroyed, the crate-on-the-mark untouched. The player's first sentence: "I cannot cross that." ~4 min (the witness phase is the longest beat — research-consistent: dread lives before the threat).
- **Act II — Discovery:** short crossings, abundant lee-cover, abort-friendly (bus-timetable law: miss a window, wait a cycle, lose nothing but time). **One short crossing's only shelter is a low-stakes beat-mark with abort cover a step away — the player REHEARSES standing on a mark through a pass before anything depends on it.** At the act's end, the occupancy rule arrives alone: condensate visibly pools and mists in an occupied lee (visual), hiss builds (audio), and the first push-out — survivable, telegraphed, phase-clamped — teaches that hiding is a window, never a state. ~3 min.
- **Act III — Escalation:** now ONE authored mutation on a stable ruleset (single-parameter law, honored in time, not just wording): the cycle gains the **half-beat stutter — visible in the lamp cadence before it is ever lethal** (old tell still true; a new rule stacks). Cover spacing widens; occupancy costs continue as taught. ~2 min.
- **Act IV — Climax:** the long crossing. No cover. One beat-mark at the midpoint — **promoted to silhouette scale: a raised ring, and the one lamp that stays lit while the cascade dies is the lamp above it (the tell itself points at the sanctuary).** The tell fires while they are exposed — and the learned, rehearsed rule demands the anti-instinct: **walk to the mark, stop, stand.** In the same shot, a hanging fixture in the lane is torn away — destruction on both sides, stillness in the middle. It passes around them. ~60–90 sec.
- **Act V — Resolution:** the exit corridor's lamps pulse once in the same cadence, further off — the place's pulse continues elsewhere, indifferent, and now legible. ~15 sec.

### 7. Choice Points (2–3, each viable but risky)
1. **Open-floor beat-riding:** cross the lanes on the count — fastest, boldest, fully exposed to a miscount.
2. **Cover-chaining:** rib to rib — slower, safer per step, but occupancy cost (the hiss) forces forward motion on a clock.
3. **Stall the machine:** a visible brake-wheel spends the room's pressure to skip ONE surge — hold-to-crank (seconds, touch-friendly), one use. **Its cost is readable before commitment: a pressure gauge beside the wheel whose needle-travel visibly IS the crank distance; the wheel sits inside a lee footprint, and crank progress persists on release** — abandoning to wait out a pass costs time, never the investment.

### 8. Information Map (Trust)
Lamp cascade = direction + countdown, always true · inhale = audio countdown, loudness-coded · scorch/debris = safe-spot map · **the crate-on-the-mark (Act I) = marks are sanctuary, demonstrated twice before it matters** · **the mark-lamp = the one light that survives the cascade, pointing at safety** · stutter appears in lamps before it is lethal · **occupancy warning is two-channel: condensate visibly pooling/misting in the recess (eyes) + hiss (ears), a full cycle before the shove** · **the shove is phase-clamped, server-side: it only ever lands at the top of a safe window, and stepping out of a lee for one beat fully resets its build** — the taught exit-and-return dance is always sufficient · the witnessed cart = the price, demonstrated. Every timing window is walkable by the slowest player. **REFUSED:** randomized schedules, hidden mid-crisis rule changes (the archive's anti-camping sin), reflex minigames (DOORS Heartbeat QTE is the named anti-pattern).

### 9. Failure Design
Swept = instant, readable death ("I moved on the wrong beat" — always self-attributable) with **per-act forward respawns, each itself a safe overlook of the relevant stretch** (an Act IV death returns to the Act III-end platform viewing the long crossing, one full cycle visible before re-entry) — the respawn vantage IS the re-lesson, and retry lands in <10 seconds from every act. No corpse-runs, no progress theft; fear is never outsourced to checkpoint cruelty (Grip's law).

### 10. Sound Design (no music)
The rhythm is machinery, mid/high band, mono-safe: rising inhale (hiss/steam) → surge (broadband roar, loudness = proximity) → settle (cooling ticks). Phase boundaries always double-carried (lamps + inhale) so the pattern is decodable eyes-only AND ears-only — the muted-phone player loses nothing. The count's downbeat is likewise two-channel: **all lamps snap to full-dark for the same half-beat the silence occupies — players learn to move on the dark-snap** (light grammar primary, so this encounter's "silence = go" never collides with The Hidden Presence's "silence = occupied"; different sound class, different sense hierarchy — set-coherence note for head review).

### 11. The Space
A machine gallery — the Threshold's airway: long hall of structural ribs, scorched lanes, no visible purpose. The impossibility: the far end is visibly farther than the building could contain. The indifference: it ran before the player existed and runs after they leave; nothing about it is aimed at anyone.

### 12. Mobile Sanity Notes
Whole-room light tells and second-scale windows are the most mobile-proof telegraph pair in the research · hold-to-crank maps to touch · high-contrast scorch/clean floor reads on a bright screen · zero reflex requirements anywhere — the bus-timetable law is intrinsically mobile-fair.

### 13. The Cut List
**Cut:** a skipped-beat fake-out at the climax (a new rule at maximum stakes violates teach-then-test) · any chasing entity variant (chase reintroduces reflexes; rhythm ≠ pursuit) · overlapping simultaneous rhythms (cognitive overload on phones; one rhythm, one mutation) · lethal cover (Hide-style ejection deaths — occupancy cost shoves, never kills) · a visible "creature" riding the surge (the process IS the threat; embodiment would invite fighting it).
